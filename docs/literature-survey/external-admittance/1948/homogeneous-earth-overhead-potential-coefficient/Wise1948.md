**Description.** Scalar self and mutual potential coefficients for infinitely long parallel wires above a flat homogeneous earth of finite conductivity and dielectric constant. The source retains an earth-related correction ``M+jN`` and states the subsequent one-wire relation between potential coefficient and capacitance.

**Expression.** Source equations (7)–(8), in the source's electromagnetic-cgs notation.

```math
p_{12}=c^2\left[2\log\frac{\rho''}{\rho'}+4(M+jN)\right],
\qquad\text{(7)}

M+jN=\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-(h+z)\xi\sqrt{\alpha}\,t}
      \cos\!\left(y\xi\sqrt{\alpha}\,t\right)}
{\sqrt{t^2+j e^{j2\eta}}+(\varepsilon-j2c\lambda\sigma)t}\,dt,
\qquad\text{(8)}

\alpha=4\pi\sigma\omega,
\qquad
\xi e^{j\eta}=\sqrt{1+j\frac{\varepsilon-1}{2c\lambda\sigma}}=s.
```

Here ``p_{12}=V_1/Q_2`` is the mutual potential coefficient, ``\rho'`` is the direct distance and ``\rho''`` the image distance shown in Fig. 1. For the self case the source writes ``p_{11}=c^2[2\log(2h/a)+4(M+jN)]`` with ``z=h``; it states that ``1/p_{11}`` is the capacitance to ground and, for two wires, ``V_1=p_{11}Q_1+p_{12}Q_2``. Those are source-provided assembly relations; this record does not independently invert a potential-coefficient matrix.

**Approximation.** Equations (7)–(8) are the source's integral representation after assigning ``\gamma=jk`` as a first approximation to longitudinal propagation. They are not full-wave expressions for an independently solved ``\gamma``. Wise's separate analytical approximation to this integral is recorded in [the companion approximation record](../homogeneous-earth-overhead-potential-coefficient-approximation/Wise1948.md).

**Limitations.** The source uses electromagnetic-cgs units and unit permeability, so its printed coefficients must not be read as SI without the source's conversion. It treats a flat homogeneous earth and infinitely long parallel thin wires. The actual output is a potential coefficient, not admittance itself. The source does not specify square-root branches beyond the displayed complex definitions.

**Reference.** [Wise1948](@cite), equations (7)–(8), printed pp. 365 and 369; definitions and propagation approximation, printed pp. 366–368.

**Transcription source.** Original publication PDF. Equations (7)–(8), their transformed equality on printed p. 369, geometry, time convention, material definitions, and the ``\gamma=jk`` statement were checked against rendered page images. PDF text extraction was used only for navigation.

## Source transcription

The source also gives the equivalent pre-scaled form on printed p. 369:

```math
M+jN=\int_0^\infty
\frac{e^{-w'v}\cos(y'v)}{\sqrt{v^2+js^2}+v/\tau^2}\,dv,
\qquad
w'=w\sqrt{\alpha},\quad y'=y\sqrt{\alpha},\quad
w=h+z,\quad \tau^2=k^2/k_2^2.
```

The paper states ``k=2\pi/\lambda`` and ``l=(v^2-k^2)^{1/2}``, ``m=(v^2-k_2^2)^{1/2}`` during the derivation. These dependencies are retained as source context and are not independent corpus formulas.

## Limitations and discrepancies

- The Markdown conversion omits the equations; the record is transcribed from page images.
- Nakagawa's later MKS restatement prints an undefined ``\mu_2`` in the second denominator factor of its equation (2) and repeats it in appendix (A-3), although only media ``0`` and ``1`` are defined. This record does not use that token to alter Wise's original.
- Nakagawa's discussant classifies the restatement as quasi-static/low-frequency because it is independent of the wire propagation constant; Nakagawa replies that comparisons show useful behavior over a wider tested range. That disagreement is evidence about the secondary restatement and does not erase Wise's explicit ``\gamma=jk`` approximation.
