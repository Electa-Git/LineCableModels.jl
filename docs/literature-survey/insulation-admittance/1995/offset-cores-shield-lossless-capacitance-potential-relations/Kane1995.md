**Description.** Lossless relations between self inductance and core-to-shield capacitance, and between mutual inductance and a dielectric potential coefficient, for cores offset inside a cylindrical shield. The source supplies these relations alongside a magnetic filament-based cable model. It does not supply a complete dielectric admittance matrix or a dielectric-loss model.

**Expression.** Complete equation (8), p. 1647, preserving the product rather than solving for a new output:

```math
\begin{aligned}
L_{ii}&=\frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}
\mathop{\mathrm{Ln}}\left[\frac{c_1^2-b_i^2}{c_1a_i}\right] \\
L_{ii}\cdot C_{ii}&=\mu_0\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{ri}.
\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(8)}
```

Equations (15),(16), p. 1648:

```math
L_{ji}=\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}
\mathop{\mathrm{Ln}}\left[
\frac{c_1^4+(b_ib_j)^2-2(b_ib_j)c_1^2\mathop{\mathrm{Cos}}(\theta_{i,j})}
{c_1^2[b_i^2+b_j^2-2b_ib_j\mathop{\mathrm{Cos}}(\theta_{i,j})]}
\right],\qquad\text{(15)}
```

```math
P_{ji}=\frac{L_{ji}}{\mu_0\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_{ri}}.
\qquad\text{(16)}
```

Lengths ``a_i,b_i,b_j,c_1`` and angle ``\theta_{i,j}`` are defined by Fig. 1 as above. ``L`` is inductance per length. Under the printed electromagnetic dimensions, ``C_{ii}`` has units ``\mathrm F/\mathrm m`` and ``P_{ji}`` has units ``\mathrm m/\mathrm F``; these latter units are equation-implied, not separately printed in (8),(16). ``\varepsilon_0`` and ``\mu_0`` are the reference constants used in these relations. The sentence directly below (8) literally calls ``\varepsilon_{ri}`` the relative permeability of the dielectric inside the shield. The epsilon notation, product, and that conflicting wording are all retained; this record does not silently replace the prose with relative permittivity.

**Approximation.** Lossless identities coupled to the paper's filament-based geometric inductances. No dielectric asymptotic expansion, discarded-loss term derivation, truncation order, or scalar-to-matrix construction is provided. “Lossless” is the author's case identification, not a reviewer-specified conductivity or loss-tangent value inserted into a more general expression.

**Limitations.** ``P_{ji}`` is not admittance, and ``C_{ii}`` is not established here as a diagonal Maxwell-capacitance-matrix entry in an arbitrary multi-core configuration. No matrix inverse, mutual capacitance ``C_{ji}``, ``Y=j\omega C`` assembly, or dielectric-loss extension is printed for these formulas. Dielectric terminology and the interpretation of its core-labelled subscript remain unresolved.

## Source transcription

Source notation and order are unchanged: (8) first, then (15),(16). The complete equation set and its geometric definitions are displayed in the formula section. The source context is preserved as follows:

- Before (8), the author says the lossless self inductance and capacitance between core ``i`` and shield are deduced from the earlier filament-loop formula (4).
- Immediately after (8), the author calls ``\varepsilon_{ri}`` relative permeability of the dielectric inside the shield. No distinct dielectric magnetic symbol is supplied there to reconcile the wording.
- Before (15), the author says the classical mutual inductance and potential coefficient follow from filament mutual formula (14) in the lossless-conductor case. Equation (15) defines the complete ``L_{ji}`` needed by (16); it is not replaced by a geometric-mean-distance estimate.
- No additional conversion follows (16). The paper returns to an added metal eddy-current impedance (17), which is part of the [internal-impedance record](../../../internal-impedance/1995/finite-shield-core-loop-skin-and-proximity-series/Kane1995.md), not a dielectric-loss relation.

The [field-region impedance record](../../../insulation-impedance/1995/offset-cores-inside-cylindrical-shield-logarithmic-field-terms/Kane1995.md) identifies the separate inductive use of (8),(15). It does not change the outputs or assembly permissions of this dielectric record.

## Evidence and approximation sources

- Fig. 1 and sections II–IV provide geometry, reference, filament construction, and the stated lossless case. These relations are explicit source-provided conversions, not reviewer-derived transformations of the metal impedance.
- Equation (8) is a self product, while (16) is a mutual potential-coefficient relation. Treating them as a complete matrix assembly would require evidence absent from the inspected four-page article.
- The four-core example on p. 1649 uses measured ``C_{ii},C_{ij}`` to compare inductances and reports approximately 10% maximum relative discrepancy at 10 MHz. That example does not itself supply the missing general capacitance/admittance matrix definition.
- The source calls the mutual inductance classical. No claim of first derivation of these dielectric relations is made from this later publication alone.

## Limitations and discrepancies

- **Printed terminology conflict:** the sentence below (8) calls the epsilon-labelled dielectric factor relative permeability. The equation is not repaired, and its displayed factor is not relabelled ``\mu_r``.
- **Unresolved material indexing:** the same ``\varepsilon_{ri}`` appears in both (8) and (16). The article does not explain a spatially varying multi-dielectric solution or reciprocal-matrix convention for different core-labelled values. No value equality or material-layer model is invented.
- **Output/assembly boundary:** the actual outputs remain a capacitance product and a potential coefficient. This record does not solve for a matrix, invert a scalar into an admittance, add ``j\omega``, or set an unprinted dielectric conductivity to zero.
- **Geometry boundary:** the mutual expression concerns distinct cores; the separate self expression is retained instead of an inferred singular self-substitution.
- **Verification/citation boundary:** equations and the conflicting prose are image-verified; citation completeness and scientific interpretation are not thereby resolved.
