**Description.** Two-logarithm approximation to Carson's overhead self and mutual ground-return impedance. It represents the homogeneous lossy earth by two complex return planes whose depths and current weights vary with the conductor-separation angle.

**Expression.** Final double-logarithmic approximation, source equation (25), with source-provided self and mutual substitutions.

```math
z=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}\left[
A\ln\frac{\sqrt{(H+2\alpha p)^2+x^2}}{D_1}
+(1-A)\ln\frac{\sqrt{(H+2\beta p)^2+x^2}}{D_1}
\right],
\qquad\text{(25)}

\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\frac{x}{H}\quad[\mathrm{deg}],

A=\begin{cases}
0.07360,&\theta\le50.45^\circ,\\
0.002474\theta-0.05127,&\theta>50.45^\circ,
\end{cases}

\alpha=\begin{cases}
0.1500,&\theta\le50.45^\circ,\\
0.004726\theta-0.08852,&\theta>50.45^\circ,
\end{cases}

\beta=\frac{1-A\alpha}{1-A},
\qquad
p=\frac{1}{\sqrt{j\omega\mu_0\sigma}}.
```

For the self term the source prescribes ``D_1=r_i``, ``H=2h_i``, ``x=0``. For the mutual term it prescribes

```math
D_1=\sqrt{(h_i-h_j)^2+x_{ij}^2},
\qquad H=h_i+h_j,
\qquad x=x_{ij}.
```

**Approximation.** Noda begins from Carson's integral (2), uses a two-logarithm form, enforces ``A+B=1`` and the infinite-frequency condition ``A\alpha+B\beta=1``, then deliberately omits the exact zero-frequency matching condition because the minimax fit is better between the limits. Piecewise-linear ``A(\theta)`` and ``\alpha(\theta)`` are least-squares fits to minimax solutions; ``B=1-A`` and ``\beta=(1-A\alpha)/(1-A)`` follow analytically. The fitted formula is an approximation, not a full-wave exact result.

**Limitations.** Homogeneous conductive nonmagnetic earth, overhead parallel wires, quasi-TEM regime, and the fitted ``\Omega,\theta`` domain. Permittivity/displacement current and an independently solved longitudinal propagation constant are absent. Reported numerical agreement is limited to the paper's distribution- and transmission-line examples.

**Reference.** [Noda2006](@cite).  T. Noda, “A Double Logarithmic Approximation of Carson's Ground-Return Impedance,” *IEEE Transactions on Power Delivery*, vol. 21, pp. 472–479, 2006, DOI `10.1109/TPWRD.2005.852307`; equations (21)–(25), printed pp. 475–476.

**Transcription source.** Original publication PDF. The parent integral, limiting conditions, fitted branches, ``\theta`` degree convention, final two logarithms, and all self/mutual substitutions were checked against rendered page images. No equation-preserving Markdown was used.

## Source transcription

The source's parent Carson expression is

```math
z=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}
\left[\ln\frac{D_2}{D_1}+I\right],
\qquad\text{(1)}

I=\int_0^\infty
\frac{2e^{-H\lambda}}{\lambda+\sqrt{\lambda^2+j\Omega}}
\cos(x\lambda)\,d\lambda,
\qquad\text{(2)}

\Omega=\omega\mu_0\sigma,
\qquad\text{(3)}
```

with ``D_2=2h_i`` for self and ``D_2=\sqrt{H^2+x_{ij}^2}`` for mutual. The paper's special-function identity (7) and ``u_1,u_2`` definitions (8) establish the limiting conditions used in the fit; they are derivational dependencies rather than a separate physical formulation.
