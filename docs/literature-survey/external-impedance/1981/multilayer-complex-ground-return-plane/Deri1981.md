**Description.** The paper computes one equivalent complex return-plane depth from the total magnetic flux in horizontally stratified conducting earth, then uses that depth in the same closed image-distance impedances as the homogeneous formulation.

**Expression.** Layer transfer, flux sum, effective depth, and its use in equations (3)–(4), transcribed in the source's notation.

```math
\frac{dE}{dx}=-j\omega\mu_0H,
\qquad
\frac{dH}{dx}=-\sigma E,
\qquad\text{(9)}

\begin{bmatrix}E_K\\H_K\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}A_K&B_K\\C_K&D_K\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}E_{K-1}\\H_{K-1}\end{bmatrix},
\qquad\text{(10)}

A_K=D_K=\frac{\theta_K+\theta_K^{-1}}{2}
=\cosh\frac{\Delta_K}{p_K},

B_K=\zeta_K\frac{\theta_K-\theta_K^{-1}}{2}
=\zeta_K\sinh\frac{\Delta_K}{p_K},

C_K=\frac{1}{\zeta_K}\frac{\theta_K-\theta_K^{-1}}{2}
=\frac{1}{\zeta_K}\sinh\frac{\Delta_K}{p_K},
\qquad\text{(11)}

p_K=\frac{1}{\sqrt{j\omega\mu_0\sigma_K}},
\qquad
\zeta_K=\sqrt{\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{\sigma_K}},
\qquad
\theta_K=e^{-\Delta_K/p_K},
\qquad\text{(12'--12''')}

\frac{E_n}{H_n}=\zeta_{n+1},
\qquad\text{(15)}

\begin{bmatrix}E_K\\H_K\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}A_{K0}&B_{K0}\\C_{K0}&A_{K0}\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}E_0\\H_0\end{bmatrix},
\qquad\text{(20)}

E_0=
\frac{\zeta_{n+1}A_{n0}-B_{n0}}
     {A_{n0}-\zeta_{n+1}C_{n0}}H_0,
\qquad\text{(21)}

\phi_K=\mu_0\int_0^{\Delta_K}H\,dx
=\frac{1}{j\omega}(E_{K-1}-E_K),
\qquad\text{(22)}

\phi=\sum_K\phi_K,
\qquad
p=\frac{\sum_K\phi_K}{\mu_0H_0}.
\qquad\text{(23--24)}
```

The resulting ``p`` is inserted without a change of notation into

```math
Z_s=j\omega\frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\ln\frac{2(h+p)}{r},
\qquad
Z_m=j\omega\frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}\ln
\frac{\sqrt{(h_k+h_\ell+2p)^2+d_{k\ell}^2}}
     {\sqrt{(h_k-h_\ell)^2+d_{k\ell}^2}}.
\qquad\text{(3--4)}
```

**Approximation.** The one-dimensional layer fields and their flux are used to construct an effective homogeneous return plane. Retaining that plane as an image surface for thin conductors is explicitly heuristic; the paper says equal complex depth is expected to produce approximately equal impedances. This is not an exact multilayer Sommerfeld solution.

**Limitations.** Horizontally stratified, nonmagnetic, conduction-dominated earth only. The printed definitions in (11)–(12) have an algebraic sign inconsistency: for the printed ``\theta_K=e^{-\Delta_K/p_K}``, ``(\theta_K-\theta_K^{-1})/2`` equals negative, not positive, ``\sinh(\Delta_K/p_K)``. This record therefore preserves the publication but does not choose an executable sign convention.

**Reference.** [Deri1981](@cite), equations (9)–(24), printed pp. 3687–3689, and equations (3)–(4), printed p. 3686.

**Transcription source.** Original publication PDF. The field equations, layer matrices, signs, exponents, boundary condition, flux terms, and depth ratio were checked visually against high-resolution renders of printed pp. 3687–3689. The Markdown conversion severely corrupts this mathematics and was not used as equation authority.

## Source transcription

The bottom layer extends to infinity and has no backward wave, giving (15). Matrix symbols ``A_{K0}``, ``B_{K0}``, and ``C_{K0}`` in (20)–(21) denote the sequential product from the surface through layer ``K``. The source sets ``H_0=I`` before calculating the layer fields and fluxes.

The discussion reply on printed p. 3693 proposes a distinct scalar-impedance evaluator,

```math
\frac{dZ}{dx}=-j\omega\mu+\sigma Z^2,
\qquad\text{(50)}

p=\frac{Z_0}{j\omega\mu_0},
\qquad\text{(51)}
```

integrated backward from ``x=\infty``. It is recorded here only as a related author-discussion formulation and is not substituted for the main paper's printed transfer matrix.

## Limitations and discrepancies

- Publication defect retained: equations (11) and (12‴) make the printed exponential difference and positive hyperbolic sine mutually inconsistent. No sign has been repaired in this record.
- Conversion defect: the Docling Markdown renders the transfer matrix and equations (9)–(24) incorrectly; page images are the authority.
- The multilayer image-plane substitution is presented as plausible/approximate; no separate universal multilayer error theorem is supplied.
