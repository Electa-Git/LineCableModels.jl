**Description.** Hybrid analytical finite-wall pipe model intended to remain usable from thin to thick pipe walls.

**Expression.**

```math
\mathbf Z=\mathbf Z_{SC}+\mathbf Z_p+\mathbf Z_0,
```

and the finite-wall input term is

```math
Z_{in}=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}\frac{\mu_0}{kR_1}
\frac{I_0(kR_1)K_1(kR_2)+K_0(kR_1)I_1(kR_2)}
{K_1(kR_1)I_1(kR_2)-I_1(kR_1)K_1(kR_2)}.
\qquad\text{(7)}
```

The source then assembles ``Z_{pjk}=Z_{in}+Q_{jk}+C_n`` with the harmonic interaction terms in (9)–(12).

**Approximation.** Hybrid finite-pipe harmonic expansion; numerical truncation of ``n`` is required.

**Numerical interpretation.** The inner-surface coefficient is evaluated in SI using the equivalent finite-wall expression in [DeSilva2019](@cite), (A6). The circular geometric harmonics are dimensionless and carry one common ``j\omega\mu_0/(2\pi)`` factor. This avoids the extra permeability factor in the printed (7) and the repeated frequency factor obtained by substituting (12) literally into (9) and (11). The printed source notation is retained.

**Limitations.** Finite wall thickness enters the pipe surface impedance, but not the positive-order eddy-current coefficients. Those coefficients retain the infinite-wall approximation; they are not the fully finite-wall response of [Fortin2005](@cite) or [DaSilva2006](@cite).

**Reference.** [Yang2001](@cite), equations (7)–(12).

**Transcription source.** Original conference-page images.

## Source transcription

``Z_p`` is classified internal because it arises from pipe wall current, while ``Z_0`` remains external earth return. The source's geometric and harmonic dependencies are:

```math
Q_{jk}=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}
\left[\ln\left(\frac{r_{p1}}{\sqrt{d_j^2+d_k^2-2d_jd_k\cos\theta_{jk}}}\right)
-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_n}{n}\right],
\qquad\text{(9)}
```

```math
Q_{jj}=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}
\ln\left[\frac{r_{p1}}{r_j}\left(1-(d_j/r_{p1})^2\right)\right],
\qquad\text{(10)}
```

```math
C_n(r,\theta)=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}\,2\mu_p
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{C_n}
{n(1+\mu_p)+X_1K_{n-1}(X_1)/K_n(X_1)},
\qquad\text{(11)}
```

```math
C_n=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}
\left(\frac{d_jd_k}{r_{p1}^2}\right)^n\cos(n\theta_{jk}).
\qquad\text{(12)}
```

Here ``d_j,d_k`` are pipe-axis offsets, ``\theta_{jk}`` is their angular separation, ``r_j`` is the enclosing radius of local cable ``j``, and ``X_1=kr_{p1}``. The same printed ``C_n`` denotes both the sum and its geometry coefficient; the numerical interpretation above separates them. The finite-wall surface plus infinite-wall harmonic construction is repeated in [DeSilva2019](@cite), (A1)–(A6).
