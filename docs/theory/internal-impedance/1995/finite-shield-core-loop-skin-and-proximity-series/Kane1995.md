**Description.** Per-unit-length self and mutual impedances of parallel round cores inside a finite-thickness conducting cylindrical shield, with the shield as return/reference conductor. The published construction adds solid-core skin and pairwise eddy-current terms to filament–shield expressions. Its complete loop output includes a logarithmic magnetic-field contribution in the nonconducting region; that contribution is not earth-return impedance or shield metal impedance.

**Expression.** The filament–shield self contribution is equation (4), p. 1647. Its three additive pieces are separately identified by the author as shield skin effect, shield proximity effect, and the field-region inductance. No terms are combined.

```math
\begin{aligned}
(R_g+j\omega L_g)I_i={}&
\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_g}\right)
\left[A_0I_0(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+B_0K_0(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right]\\
&+\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_g}\right)
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{b_i}{c_1}\right)^n
\left[A_nI_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+B_nK_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right]\\
&+j\omega\frac{\mu_0 I_i}{2\pi}
\mathop{\mathrm{Ln}}\left[\frac{c_1^2-b_i^2}{c_1a_i}\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(4)}
```

Boundary coefficients, (5),(6), apply also at ``n=0`` with the definitions immediately following them:

```math
\begin{aligned}
A_n={}&\frac{I_i\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}{2\pi\Delta}
(P_n+Q_n)
\left[\frac{\mu_{rg}n}{c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}
K_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+K'_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right],
\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(5)}
```

```math
\begin{aligned}
B_n={}&-\frac{I_i\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}{2\pi\Delta}
(P_n+Q_n)
\left[\frac{\mu_{rg}n}{c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}
I_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+I'_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(6)}
```

```math
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=\frac{1}{c_1} \\
Q_0&=0;
\qquad n>0: \\
P_n&=\frac{1}{c_1}\left(\frac{b_i}{c_1}\right)^n \\
Q_n&=\frac{1}{c_1}\left(\frac{b_i}{c_1}\right)^n.
\end{aligned}
```

Both ``P_n`` and ``Q_n`` have the same printed positive-order expression. The denominator is printed as ``\Delta`` without an order subscript, although it contains ``n``. Its unnumbered definition begins at the bottom of the left column and continues at the top of the right column:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\Delta={}&
\left[\frac{\mu_{rg}n}{c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}
I_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
-I'_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right]\\
&\quad\cdot
\left[\frac{\mu_{rg}n}{c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}
K_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+K'_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right]\\
&-
\left[\frac{\mu_{rg}n}{c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}
I_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+I'_n(c_2\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right]\\
&\quad\cdot
\left[\frac{\mu_{rg}n}{c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g}}
K_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
-K'_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right].
\end{aligned}
```

``I_n,K_n`` are explicitly defined immediately below (6) as modified Bessel functions of the first and second kind, order ``n``. Prime notation is printed but not explicitly defined in the article; interpretation as differentiation with respect to the displayed argument is conventional, not an author-stated definition recovered here. The square-root branch is not stated. ``I_i`` in the coefficient prefactors is the exciting current, not a Bessel function.

The inner shield surface contribution is separately isolated by the source:

```math
(R_s+j\omega L_s)=(R_g+j\omega L_g)
-j\omega\frac{\mu_0}{2\pi}
\mathop{\mathrm{Ln}}\left[\frac{c_1^2-b_i^2}{c_1a_i}\right].
\qquad\text{(7)}
```

For ``b_i=0``, the prose identifies (7) with the skin-effect surface impedance credited to “Shelkunhoff [12]”; the reference-number inconsistency is retained below. No separate outer-surface or through-wall transfer expression is supplied in this paper.

The solid-core contribution including the other-core increments is (9):

```math
z'_{iii}=\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_i}\right)
\left[\frac{\sqrt{j\omega\mu_i\sigma_i}}{2\pi a_i}
\frac{I_0(a_i\sqrt{j\omega\mu_i\sigma_i})}
{I_1(a_i\sqrt{j\omega\mu_i\sigma_i})}\right]
+\sum_{\substack{k=1\\k\ne i}}^{P}z'_{iki}.
\qquad\text{(9)}
```

Each other-core term is printed in the following unsimplified form. In particular, the two distance powers and the separate power ``a_k^{n-1}`` are retained:

```math
\begin{aligned}
z'_{iki}={}&\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_k}\right)
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{j\omega\mu_0\sigma_k a_k^{n-1}}{\pi}
\left(\frac{a_k}{b_{ik}}\right)^n
\left[\left(\frac{1}{b_{ik}}\right)^n\right]
I_n(a_k\sqrt{j\omega\mu_k\sigma_k})\\
&\quad\cdot\left[
\frac{1}{
\left(\frac{n}{a_k}\right)I_n(a_k\sqrt{j\omega\mu_k\sigma_k})
+\left(\frac{\sqrt{j\omega\mu_k\sigma_k}}{\mu_{rk}}\right)
I'_n(a_k\sqrt{j\omega\mu_k\sigma_k})}
\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(10)}
```

The source assembly, first line of (11), is:

```math
Z_{i,i\mathrm{Tot}}=(R_{i,i\mathrm{Tot}}+j\omega L_{i,i\mathrm{Tot}})
=(R_g+j\omega L_g)+z'_{iii}.
\qquad\text{(11, first line)}
```

The expanded printed form is retained in the source-transcription section. The label “Tot” therefore does not mean isolated shield or isolated core impedance.

For excitation of core ``i`` and response of core ``j``, the filament mutual expression is (14), with the same ``A_n,B_n`` generated by ``I_i,b_i``:

```math
\begin{aligned}
(R_{j,i}+j\omega L_{j,i})I_i={}&
\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_g}\right)
\left[A_0I_0(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+B_0K_0(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right]\\
&+\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_g}\right)
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{b_j}{c_1}\right)^n
\left[A_nI_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})
+B_nK_n(c_1\sqrt{j\omega\mu_g\sigma_g})\right]
\cdot\mathop{\mathrm{Cos}}(n\theta_{i,j})\\
&+j\omega\frac{\mu_0 I_i}{4\pi}
\mathop{\mathrm{Ln}}\left[
\frac{c_1^4+(b_ib_j)^2-2(b_ib_j)c_1^2\mathop{\mathrm{Cos}}(\theta_{i,j})}
{c_1^2[b_i^2+b_j^2-2b_ib_j\mathop{\mathrm{Cos}}(\theta_{i,j})]}
\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(14)}
```

The additional mutual eddy-current term due to conductor ``i`` in conductor ``j`` is (17), p. 1648. Its source structure is retained, with commuting factors displayed in the corpus's ``j\omega\mu\sigma`` order:

```math
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\mathrm{ind},j}={}&\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_j}\right)
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{j\omega\mu_0\sigma_j a_j^{n-1}}{\pi}
I_n(a_j\sqrt{j\omega\mu_j\sigma_j})
\left(\frac{a_j}{b_{ij}}\right)^n
\cdot\left[\left(\frac{1}{b_{ij}}\right)^n\right]\\
&\quad\cdot\left[
\frac{1}{
\left(\frac{n}{a_j}\right)I_n(a_j\sqrt{j\omega\mu_j\sigma_j})
+\left(\frac{\sqrt{j\omega\mu_j\sigma_j}}{\mu_{rj}}\right)
I'_n(a_j\sqrt{j\omega\mu_j\sigma_j})}
\right].
\end{aligned}\qquad\text{(17)}
```

```math
Z_{j,i\mathrm{Tot}}=(R_{j,i\mathrm{Tot}}+j\omega L_{j,i\mathrm{Tot}})
=(R_{j,i}+j\omega L_{j,i})+Z_{\mathrm{ind},j}.
\qquad\text{(18)}
```

The concluding sentence instructs repeating the calculation for ``Z_{i,j\mathrm{Tot}}`` and literally prints ``(I_i=0,\ I_i\ne0)``. These simultaneous conditions are contradictory; no corrected index or imposed symmetry is supplied here.

Required material definitions are those stated in section II:

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mu_i&=\mu_0\mu_{ri} \\
\mu_g&=\mu_0\mu_{rg}.
\end{aligned}
```

The corresponding ``j,k`` core labels in (10),(17) denote the source's other cores. Lengths are in metres, conductivities in ``\mathrm{S/m}``, permeabilities in ``\mathrm{H/m}``, relative permeabilities dimensionless. The prose below (10) nevertheless calls ``\mu_k`` relative permeability; this conflicts with the absolute/relative distinction just stated and is not normalized away. The source provides no source-independent scattering coefficients or recursion terminal conditions beyond the displayed infinite-series representation.

**Approximation.** The finite shield is not replaced by an infinite external radius. The paper constructs filament–shield fields, adds a solid-core skin contribution and the displayed pairwise eddy-current increments, and says it retains the “most important” proximity effects. It does not give an expansion parameter, retained interaction order, discarded interaction list, or error bound. This is a finite-shield skin/proximity model, not an exact full multi-core field solution. Section V discusses Bessel recurrences for evaluation but supplies neither recurrence equations nor starting/terminal formulas.

**Limitations.** Root branch and prime definitions are not explicit; the reverse-excitation condition is contradictory; conductor permeability terminology and one reference number conflict. No arbitrary terminal-network transformation, outer-surface/transfer term, dielectric-loss model, or earth return is supplied. Numerical comparisons concern the source's specified cable examples only.

**Reference.** [Kane1995](@cite).  Kane, Ahmad and Auriol (1995), “Multiwire Shielded Cable Parameter Computation,” IEEE Transactions on Magnetics 31(3), pp. 1646–1649; sections II–IV, equations (4)–(18), with (8),(15),(16) documented separately as dielectric-region formulas.

**Transcription source.** Original-publication page images in the HAL copy, printed pp. 1646–1649/PDF pages 2–5. Full pages and enlarged crops of (10),(11),(14)–(18), including the reverse-excitation sentence, were visually inspected. No Markdown enrichment or later-author equation is substituted for the original.

## Evidence and approximation sources

- Original title, byline, May 1995 running header, all four article pages, and reference list were image-inspected. The repository cover's 2007 deposit is not a publication-year substitution.
- Section II supplies the conductor constitutive/field model. Section III first constructs a filament loop and then adds solid-core skin plus eddy-current contributions from the other ``P-1`` cores. Section IV constructs a filament mutual loop and then adds (17). Infinite sums and all factors are preserved, without substituting the later Høidalen Bessel-ratio representation.
- The author credits core eddy-current contributions to reference [13], which the original reference list identifies as Schelkunoff's 1934 Bell System Technical Journal article. That is an attribution, not proof that its specific equations occur in that earlier article. Høidalen later questions the attribution and modifies the contribution; see the [separate later-author record](../../2013/solid-core-proximity-low-frequency-subtracted-series/Hoidalen2013.md).
- The author calls the ``b_i=0`` case of (7) a known surface impedance, but cites [12] while naming Shelkunhoff. The original reference list identifies [12] as Kane's July 1994 ECL thesis and [13] as Schelkunoff. No reference number is silently corrected.
- Section V describes backward recurrence for ``I_n`` and forward recurrence for ``\mathop{\mathrm{ker}}_n,\mathop{\mathrm{kei}}_n``, and calculation of ``K_n`` through the latter. It refers to a starting order and a normalizing factor but does not print their formulae, any recurrence, or a terminal condition. These are unfulfilled evaluation-reference leads, not an extracted algorithm or an invitation to invent one.
- Accuracy evidence is example-specific: Table I compares one three-core self resistance against FEM; the four-core comparison at 10 MHz reports approximately 10% maximum relative discrepancy against inductances based on measured capacitances. For the eccentric one-core example ``c_1=5`` mm, ``c_2=7`` mm, ``b_1=3`` mm, ``a_1=1`` mm, nonmagnetic copper shield, the author reports maximum relative shield-resistance discrepancy of 12% at 10 MHz (p. 1649, Table II). These are not analytic error bounds for arbitrary geometries or materials.
- No original-article Markdown was available for a direct conversion comparison. PDF text extraction lost equations, misread geometric values and reference typography, and was not used to settle mathematics.

## Limitations and discrepancies

- **Demonstrable printed contradiction:** section IV's reverse-excitation sentence prints ``I_i=0`` and ``I_i\ne0`` together. The image was enlarged and both subscripts checked. An intended replacement index is not supplied by this record, and mutual symmetry is not assumed.
- **Printed definition conflict:** section II distinguishes absolute ``\mu_i`` from relative ``\mu_{ri}``, while prose below (10) calls ``\mu_k`` relative permeability. Both usages remain visible; the radicals and explicit ``\mu_{rk}`` denominator are unchanged.
- **Printed citation mismatch:** the named Shelkunhoff surface-impedance attribution following (7) points to [12], but the reference list's Schelkunoff entry is [13]. The core-proximity attribution really does point to [13]; neither attribution establishes equation-level historical attribution without comparison to the cited source.
- **Unspecified original definitions, later corroboration distinguished:** root branch and prime differentiation variable are not explicit in the article. The Bessel kinds themselves are explicit and verified. The later da Silva–Fernández–Rivas (2006) conference nomenclature, English p. 1, explicitly defines the corresponding primes as first derivatives; see its [finite-pipe record](../../2006/finite-pipe-without-core-proximity-auxiliary-model/DaSilva2006.md). That is later-witness corroboration, not a newly discovered definition in Kane's original or an algebraic equivalence proof. The field-contour equalities are retained without a reviewer-supplied normalization.
- **Model completeness not proved:** the paper claims its “most important” proximity effects and supplies a pairwise addition, without a stated interaction expansion/truncation order. The record does not label this an exact solution of every conducting-core interaction.
- **No source-provided outer/transfer or earth formula:** finite ``c_2`` in the coefficients is not evidence that the paper supplies a separate outer-surface or through-wall terminal matrix. No such expression is fabricated.
- **No isolated-term singularity finding:** inverse currents and Bessel factors are part of complete linear-response expressions. Their appearance alone is not used to claim physical singularities or to insert zero-frequency replacements.

## Numerical interpretation and implementation

`PipeImpedance.Formula(:Kane1995)` reuses the finite-wall boundary
response of the DaSilva2006 implementation and adds the separate
pairwise core contribution. Individual core skin impedance belongs
to the coaxial block and is added only once. The hollow-region
logarithm uses the outer insulation boundary when radial insulation
is represented separately; the sum then reproduces the source's
metal-radius field logarithm.

The derivative recurrence reduces (10) and (17) to the shared
directional coefficient

```math
\Delta Z_k(d)=\frac{j\omega\mu_0}{2\pi}
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}
\frac{2\mu_{rk}(a_k/d)^{2n}}
{n(\mu_{rk}-1)+z_k I_{n-1}(z_k)/I_n(z_k)},
\qquad
z_k=a_k\sqrt{j\omega\mu_0\mu_{rk}\sigma_k}.
```

This is an algebraic restatement, also printed by [Hoidalen2013](@cite),
not an additional physical formula. The square root has positive
real part for positive frequency and follows conjugacy for negative
frequency. Finite sums use a stated tolerance and report nonconvergence.

The source's unequal-core mutual expressions are directional. The
reciprocal pipe-matrix selection therefore requires equal solid-core
radii, conductivities, and permeabilities, with screenless coaxial
units. It does not average unequal mutual coefficients. The separate
`core_proximity(Val(:Kane1995), ...)` evaluator retains the directional
pair term for source comparison. These restrictions preserve the
published approximation without claiming a multiple-scattering solution.
