Finite element solver

The Gmsh extension computes the series impedances and shunt admittances of a problem by means of the finite-element method. The cross-section of the system is built and meshed by Gmsh, and each frequency is solved by GetDP. The matrices of the terminals are then reduced in the same way as in the coaxial computation.

Loading the extension

The extension is loaded by using Gmsh, and the Julia package Gmsh.jl provides the Gmsh library with it. The field equations are solved by GetDP 3.5.0 in complex arithmetic, an external executable that the extension finds or installs as described below. The finite-element computation is tested on Linux.

GetDP executable

The computation option getdp_executable selects the executable by a GetdpBinary choice or by a path:

ChoiceExecutable
:discover, the defaultthe getdp found on PATH when its output of getdp -info reports version 3.5 and complex arithmetic, and otherwise the executable of :download
:downloadthe GetDP 3.5.0 complex build installed as an artifact of the package
a path, given as a stringthe executable at that path, used as is

Any other symbol throws an ArgumentError. With :download, or with :discover when no suitable GetDP is found on PATH, the extension installs the lazy artifact getdp of the Artifacts.toml of the package at its first use. Pkg then downloads the archive of the platform and verifies its SHA-256 hash and its tree hash. The archive is unpacked under the artifacts/ folder of the Julia depot. Only this first use requires network access: later computations run the installed copy, which stays in the depot until Pkg.gc() removes it. Since :discover looks at PATH before every computation, a suitable GetDP installed later is used in place of the artifact.

PlatformArchive
Linux on x86-64, glibcgetdp-3.5.0-Linux64c.tgz of getdp.info
macOS on x86-64 and on Apple silicongetdp-3.5.0-MacOSXc.tgz of getdp.info, an x86-64 build that Apple silicon runs through Rosetta 2
Windows on x86-64getdp-3.5.0-Windows64c.tar.gz, the files of the getdp.info build repacked as a tarball in the release getdp-3.5.0 of the package

On another platform, or on a machine without network access, GetDP 3.5 with complex arithmetic is installed separately and given on PATH or by its path.

Before each computation, the extension runs the selected executable with -info and requires an output that names GetDP and a version number. Otherwise, it throws an error. The path of the executable must be shorter than 256 bytes, a limit of the native path arguments. The file logs/getdp-info.log of the run directory records the executable, the choice and the output of -info.

Since the trait backend of FEM is GmshBackend, the call compute(problem, FEM()) runs the extension.

Field equations

The finite element model solves the fields of the cross-section with the dependence $e^{\mathrm{j}\omega t - \Gamma z}$, where $z$ is the axial coordinate, $x$ and $y$ are the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the cross-section, and the interface between air and earth lies at $y = 0$. Each region has the complex conductivity $\widehat\sigma = \sigma + \mathrm{j}\omega\varepsilon$ and the reluctivity $\nu = 1/\mu$. A conductor takes the admittivity $\kappa = 1/\rho(\theta) + \mathrm{j}\omega\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r$, with $\rho(\theta)$ from the resistivity law at the operating temperature, and a dielectric the admittivity of its law. Both then have $\sigma = \operatorname{Re}\kappa$ and $\varepsilon = \operatorname{Im}\kappa/\omega$. The air and the earth take $\sigma = 1/\rho$, $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r$ and $\mu = \mu_0\mu_r$ from the law of the earth properties.

The fields are expressed by the normalized potentials $a$, $\mathbf b$ and $v$, with $A_z = a$, $\mathbf A_t = \Gamma\mathbf b$ and $\phi = \Gamma v$, in the framework of the potential formulation of [5]. The normalization retains every $\Gamma^2$ term and has a regular limit for $\Gamma = 0$:

\[\mathbf E_t = -\Gamma\left(\nabla_t v + \mathrm{j}\omega\mathbf b\right), \qquad E_z = \Gamma^2 v - \mathrm{j}\omega a, \qquad \mathbf B_t = \nabla_t \times (a\hat{\mathbf z}) - \Gamma^2\hat{\mathbf z} \times \mathbf b.\]

Outside the metal, the axial component of Ampère's law, its transverse component divided by $\Gamma$, and the continuity of the current divided by $\Gamma$ give

\[\begin{aligned} \hat{\mathbf z} \cdot \nabla_t \times (\nu\mathbf B_t) &= \widehat\sigma E_z, \\ \nabla_t \times (\nu\nabla_t \times \mathbf b) + (\mathrm{j}\omega\widehat\sigma - \Gamma^2\nu)\mathbf b + \widehat\sigma\nabla_t v - \nu\nabla_t a &= 0, \\ \nabla_t \cdot \left[\widehat\sigma\left(\nabla_t v + \mathrm{j}\omega\mathbf b\right)\right] + \widehat\sigma\left(\Gamma^2 v - \mathrm{j}\omega a\right) &= 0. \end{aligned}\]

In the metal of terminal $k$, the axial field is $E_z = -\mathrm{j}\omega a - w_k$, with one unknown $w_k$ per terminal, and the axial equation holds with the total current

\[\int_{\Omega_k} \widehat\sigma E_z \, \mathrm{d}\Omega = I_k.\]

The transverse fields are solved outside the metal only. The contour of each terminal is an equipotential electrode at the floating potential $V_k$, with $\mathbf b \times \mathbf n = 0$, and its outward leakage current per unit length is $\Gamma I_k$, which follows from the continuity of the axial current. The physical axial drive of the terminal is $w_k + \Gamma^2 V_k$. The outer boundary of the perfectly matched layer has $a = 0$, $\mathbf b \times \mathbf n = 0$ and $v = 0$, and a tree gauge, started on the conductor contours and on the outer boundary, removes the gradient part of $\mathbf b$. GetDP solves the Galerkin form of these equations with first-order nodal functions for $a$ and $v$ and edge functions for $\mathbf b$, by the direct solver MUMPS. Each column $j$ of the solution imposes $I_j = 1$ A on terminal $j$ and zero on the other terminals, in both the axial current and the leakage.

Line parameters from the fields

The voltage of terminal $i$ divided by $\Gamma$ is the integral of the normalized transverse field along a vertical line $\ell_i$, from the interface for a terminal in the air, or from the outer boundary of the bottom layer for a terminal in the earth, to the metal of terminal $i$. With the excitation of column $j$,

\[P_{ij} = \frac{1}{I_j} \int_{\ell_i} \left(\nabla_t v + \mathrm{j}\omega\mathbf b\right) \cdot \mathrm{d}\boldsymbol\ell, \qquad K_{ij} = -\frac{w_i + \Gamma^2 V_i}{I_j}, \qquad Z_{ij} = K_{ij} + \Gamma^2 P_{ij}, \qquad \mathbf Y = \mathbf P^{-1},\]

where $w_i$ and $V_i$ are the axial drive and the floating potential of terminal $i$ in column $j$, $P$ is in Ω·m, $K$ and $Z$ in Ω/m, and $Y$ in S/m. The potential coefficients of the coaxial computation correspond to $\mathrm{j}\omega P$. The starting points of the lines reproduce the voltage references of the unified earth-return formula: the interface for a conductor in the air, and infinite depth for a conductor in the earth. Each line is shifted horizontally by $\min(10^{-5}, 0.01 d_i)$ m from the anchor of the terminal, where $d_i$ is the smallest metal dimension of the terminal, and the metal interiors add nothing to the integral. The matrices are then reduced by bundling, Kron elimination and ideal transposition, as in the coaxial computation.

Domain and perfectly matched layers

For each medium $m$, the base root $q_{0,m} = \sqrt{\mathrm{j}\omega\mu_m\widehat\sigma_m} = a_{0,m} + \mathrm{j} b_{0,m}$ has a nonnegative real part, and the transverse root $q_m = a_m + \mathrm{j} b_m$ of $q_m^2 = \mathrm{j}\omega\mu_m\widehat\sigma_m - \Gamma^2$ has $b_m \geq 0$ when $\operatorname{Im}\Gamma < b_{0,m}$ and $a_m \geq 0$ otherwise. The layout radius $R_l$ is the largest of 5 m, of the distance from $(x_c, 0)$ to each cable plus its radius, and of twice the distance between each pair of cables, where $x_c$ is the mean horizontal position of the cables. The sizing length of the earth $L$ and the half-width $D$ of the physical domain $|x - x_c| \leq D$, $|y| \leq D$ are

\[L_0 = \min\left(\frac{1}{a_{0,e}}, \frac{2\pi}{|q_{0,e}|}\right), \qquad L = \min\left(L_0, \sqrt{\frac{2\rho_\mathrm{cap}}{\omega\mu_e}}\right), \qquad D = \max\left(R_l, m_D L \frac{|q_{0,e}|}{\max(|q_{0,e}|, |q_e|)}\right),\]

where the index $e$ denotes the earth, $1/a_{0,e}$ is omitted for $a_{0,e} = 0$, $\rho_\mathrm{cap} = 10^5$ Ω·m, and $m_D$ is domain_size_factor. The side, top and bottom layers have the thicknesses $L_d = f_d D$, with $f_d$ the overrides PmlSideThicknessFactor, PmlTopThicknessFactor and PmlBottomThicknessFactor.

Each layer stretches its normal coordinate by the staged profile

\[s_d(u) = 1 + A_d\left(u^3 - \mathrm{j}\frac{9}{4}\eta_d u^8\right),\]

where $u$ is the normalized depth in the layer, $(|x - x_c| - D)/L_d$ on the sides and $(|y| - D)/L_d$ at the top and bottom. The real part starts cubically, and the absorption of propagating waves is delayed to the eighth degree. The stretches enter the materials as $\nu\mathbf T^{-1}$ and $\widehat\sigma\mathbf T$ in the transverse terms and as $\widehat\sigma s_x s_y$ in the axial terms, with $\mathbf T = \operatorname{diag}(s_y/s_x, s_x/s_y, s_x s_y)$, and the side stretch is the same in both media, which keeps the interface matched. The layers of the sides use both media, the top layer the air and the bottom layer the earth, denoted by the sets $M_d$. The imaginary stretch and the strength of each layer are

\[\begin{aligned} \eta_d &= \min\left(\max_{m \in M_d} \nu_m, \min_{m \in M_d} \beta_m\right), \qquad \nu_m = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\frac{b_m - a_m}{b_m}, 0, 1\right), \qquad \beta_m = \min\left(1, \frac{2 a_m}{9 |b_m|}\right), \\ A_d &= \frac{4 T}{L_d \min_{m \in M_d} \widehat d_{m,d}}, \qquad \widehat d_{m,d} = \max\left(\frac{a_m + \eta_d b_m}{C_m}, 0.1 b_{0,m}\right), \qquad C_m = \frac{a_{0,m} + b_{0,m}}{b_{0,m}}, \qquad T = -\frac{\ln R}{2}, \end{aligned}\]

where $\nu_m = 0$ for $b_m \leq 0$, $\beta_m = 1$ for $b_m \geq 0$, and $R$ is pml_reflection. When the air field is quasi-static over 100 half-widths, $100 |q_{0,a}| D \leq 0.1$, and the rate of a layer is below $T / (100 D)$, the layer takes $\eta_d = 0$ and the rate $\max(T / (100 D), a_m / C_m, 0.1 b_{0,m})$, which limits the added real stretch to 100 half-widths. With $X_d = L_d(1 + A_d/4 - \mathrm{j}\eta_d A_d/4)$, the net exponent $\operatorname{Re}[q_m(D + X_d)]$ must be positive in every medium of each layer, except at the exact cutoff $q_m = 0$. Otherwise, GetDP stops with an error.

Mesh

The number of intervals across layer $d$ and their progression $e^{g_d/N_d}$ are

\[N_d = \max\left(N_{\min,d}, \left\lceil \frac{\max_{m \in M_d} \mathrm{PPW}_m \Phi_{m,d}}{2\pi} \right\rceil\right), \qquad \Phi_{m,d} = |q_m||X_d| \min\left(1, \frac{T}{E_{m,d}}\right), \qquad E_{m,d} = \operatorname{Re}(q_m X_d),\]

where $\Phi_{m,d} = |q_m||X_d|$ for $E_{m,d} \leq 0$, $\mathrm{PPW}_m = 10 \operatorname{clamp}\left(\sqrt{0.1|q_m|/a_m}, 1, 3\right)/m_h$ for $a_m > 0$ and $30/m_h$ otherwise, $m_h$ is mesh_size_factor, $N_{\min,d}$ are the overrides PmlSideLayers, PmlTopLayers and PmlBottomLayers, and $g_d$ are PmlSideGrading, PmlTopGrading and PmlBottomGrading. The air takes part only when its field is excited, which happens when a cable lies in the air or when the exterior of a buried cable lies within $6/a_e$ of the interface, and always for $a_e = 0$.

The physical regions are meshed with triangles and the layers with quadrangles. With the resolution radius $R_r = \max(R_l, L)$, the bulk target $h_b$, the remote target $h_r$, the wave target $h_{w,m}$ and the decay radius $r_{d,m}$ of each medium are

\[h_b = \frac{m_h R_r}{20}, \qquad h_r = \min\left(f_\mathrm{ext} h_b, h_b + 0.2 m_h \max(0, D - 2 R_r)\right), \qquad h_{w,m} = \min\left(h_b, \frac{m_h}{8 |q_m|}\right), \qquad r_{d,m} = \min\left(2 R_r, \frac{6}{a_m}\right),\]

where $f_\mathrm{ext}$ is ExteriorMeshSizeFactor and $6/a_m$ is omitted for $a_m \leq 0$. In a lossy earth, the exterior size grows with the distance $d$ from the cables as $h(d) = \min(h_{r,e}, h_{w,e} e^{a_e d / 2})$. A mesh boundary layer on the earth side of the interface has the first size $h_{w,e}$, the growth ratio 1.4 and the thickness $\min(r_{d,e}, \frac{1}{2}\min_p(|y_p| - r_p - h_p))$ over the buried cables $p$, with $h_p$ the target of the neighbourhood of the cable. The layer is used when $h_{w,e} < h_{r,e}$ and its thickness is at least $2 h_{w,e}$.

A circular arc of radius $R$ takes at least $\max(n_c, 2\pi R/h)$ segments per turn, with $h$ the target of its region and $n_c = 12 \cdot 2^{\max(0, \lceil \log_2(\sqrt{2\pi^2/(3\tau)}/12) \rceil)}$, which bounds the relative error of the area of a circle by $\tau$, the override ConductorGeometryTolerance. Each region has the target $h$ equal to $m_h$ times $r/5$ for a solid circle of radius $r$, $r$ for a round strand, $(r_o - r_i)/2$ for an annulus, $\min(w, h_\square)/5$ for a rectangle of width $w$ and height $h_\square$, and $\min(w, h_\square)/2$ for a rectangular strand. A thin passive annulus is meshed as a strip of $\max(2, \lceil 2/m_h \rceil)$ radial intervals, with the target of the region at least $2\pi r_o/n_c$.

In a circular or annular conductor of width $w$, the radius of a circle or the thickness of an annulus, the skin depth $\delta$ and the phase rate $\beta$ follow from $\sqrt{\mathrm{j}\omega\mu\widehat\sigma} = 1/\delta + \mathrm{j}\beta$. A graded layer of elements along the contour of the conductor, of depth $e$ and $N$ intervals growing by the ratio $g$, starts at the first size $h_1$:

\[e = \min(n_s\delta, f w), \qquad N = \left\lceil \frac{\ln\left(1 + (g - 1) e / h_1^\ast\right)}{\ln g} \right\rceil, \qquad h_1 = \frac{e (g - 1)}{g^N - 1}, \qquad h_1^\ast = \min\left(\frac{m_h\delta}{n_\delta}, \frac{m_h w}{n_t}\right),\]

where $n_s$, $n_\delta$, $g$ and, for an annulus, $n_t$ are the overrides ConductorSkinDepths, ConductorSkinDepthElements, ConductorMeshGrowth and ConductorThicknessElements, $n_t = 5$ for a circle, and $f$ is 0.5 for a circle and 0.45 for an annulus. The bent rectangular strands keep the target of their region. The interior of the conductor has the target $\min(h, m_h w / n_t)$, further limited to $2\pi m_h / (12\beta)$ when $w / \delta \leq n_s$.

Run directory

Each computation writes its inputs and outputs under the current directory, in .linecablemodels/fem/<system id>/<yyyymmdd-HHMMSS>-<4 random characters>/. The run directory contains the native sources and the geometry under input/, the meshes under mesh/, the matrices results/Z.tsv and results/P.tsv, and the logs under logs/, where logs/getdp-info.log records the executable used in the computation. With the default keep_run_directory = false, the run directory is removed after the result is built. With keep_run_directory = true, the directory is kept and its path is logged. When a native process fails, the run directory is kept, and the error message gives the frequency and the log file with its last twenty lines.

Example

The reference case two_bare_wires consists of two bare copper wires, buried at 1 m depth with a horizontal spacing of 1 m, in a homogeneous earth of 0.1 Ω·m. The example below computes this system at three frequencies of its range.

Build the system and compute its line parameters:

using LineCableModels, Gmsh
copper = Material(:conductor, 1.7241e-8, 1, 0.999994, 20, 0.00393) # [Ω·m], [°C], [1/°C]
wire = build(CableDesign, "bare_wire",
    Terminal(:core, Region(:core_metal, Circle(0.0425), copper))) # [m]
system = build(CableSystem, [wire, wire], [Placement(0.0, -1.0), Placement(1.0, -1.0)];
    connections = [(core = 1,), (core = 2,)], id = "two_bare_wires", line_length = 1.0)
frequencies = (10.0 .^ range(-1, 7; length = 101))[[1, 51, 101]] # [Hz]
problem = LineParametersProblem(system, homogeneous(rho = 0.1); frequencies) # [Ω·m]
result = compute(problem, FEM())
result.Z[1, 1, :] # [Ω/m]

Accuracy

The test suite compares the finite-element results with the analytical ones for the six reference cases, at each frequency, by means of the relative errors

\[e_Z = \frac{\max_{ij} |\Delta Z_{ij}|}{\max_i |Z_{ii}|}, \qquad e_Y = \frac{\max_{ij} |\Delta Y_{ij}|}{\max_i |Y_{ii}|}.\]

The accuracy level of the finite-element computation is 1 %. The maximum errors over the eleven finite-element frequencies of each case are:

Case$e_Z$$e_Y$
two_bare_wires0.568 %0.798 %
two_insulated_wires0.407 %0.0447 %
coaxial_circular_strands4.92 %10.5 %
coaxial_rectangular_strands5.22 %10.4 %
overhead_bare_wires0.610 %0.817 %
mixed_bare_wires0.609 %0.617 %

The differences in the coaxial cases result from the different representations of the cable in the two models:

  • $e_Y$: the analytical model represents the armor wires as a tube. In the finite-element model, the wires touch the bedding of 3 mm only at their crowns, with a filler of relative permittivity 1 in the wedges between them.
  • $e_Z$ below about 25 Hz: the lay correction of the armor is included in the equivalent conductor of the analytical model and absent from the two-dimensional model.
  • $e_Z$ above about 40 kHz: the lay correction of the core, for the same reason.
  • $e_Z$ from 25 Hz to 40 kHz: the magnetic armor wires compared with the equivalent tube.

For the four wire cases, the finite-element results of this package agree with those of the reference computation within 1e-6 of the largest diagonal entry.

Continuous integration

The workflow .github/workflows/FEM.yml runs the complete test suite, including the finite-element tests of the reference cases, on every pull request that is not a draft.

Uncertain inputs

For any number type of the problem, the backend computes the nominal problem, in which each number is replaced by the Float64 value of its nominal value, and returns a LineParameters{Float64} without uncertainty. The propagation of uncertainties is performed by the coaxial computation, as described in Uncertainty quantification.
