Earth return

Physical model

The air above the interface y = 0 and the earth below it are homogeneous half-spaces, each with its resistivity $\rho$, relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$ and relative permeability $\mu_r$. A layered earth enters as its equivalent earth, which Properties of the air and the earth describes. Each cable is a source of circumferential currents on its exterior circle, which lies wholly in one medium. A pair of cables lies in the air, in the earth or across the interface, the mixed domain.

An earth-return formula gives the earth-return impedance Zg, the earth-return potential coefficients Pg, or both, per pair of cables and from their exterior surfaces. They are the matrices $\mathbf Z_{\mathrm{env}}$ and $\mathbf P_{\mathrm{env}}$ of the matrix formulation, added to every pair of conductors of each pair of cables. Each formula treats the domains that Line parameters lists.

The default formula, :martins2026, treats the three domains and gives both quantities. It closes the circumferential currents of all cables completely through its current matrix $\mathbf T$. The fields vary along the line as $e^{-\Gamma x}$, with a prescribed longitudinal wavenumber $\Gamma$, zero by default.

The other formulas are classical expressions of the literature, each with the assumptions that its block below states. They take each cable as a line source at its center, with the current matrix $\mathbf T = \mathbf I$. Of these, :xue2018 alone reads the prescribed $\Gamma$.

The fields of each medium are spectral integrals over the real line of the transverse wavenumber. A spectral formula evaluates them on a complex path. The constructor of SpectralIntegral chooses the path with the breakpoints of the quadrature, and integrate evaluates each integral by adaptive quadrature. The option integral of the formula selects the method and its controls, integral = (method = :quad, rtol, atol, maxevals).

Validity of the default formula

The default formula, :martins2026, holds within two ranges.

  • Prescribed Γ. A nonzero Γ lies between the propagation constants of the two media. It is in range at a frequency where Im γair ≤ Im Γ ≤ Im γearth and Re Γ ≤ Re γ_earth. Γ = 0, the default, always lies in range.
  • Thick receiver. The formula represents each receiving conductor by the mean field on its exterior circumference. A conductor that is thick compared with the transverse wavelength of its medium receives a voltage that departs from the full-field value. The error grows as (κm rp)², where κm is the outgoing root of γm² − Γ² in the medium of conductor p and rp is its exterior radius, the jacket for an insulated cable. The range is |κm r_p| ≤ 0.1. Emission from a thick conductor is accurate.

Properties of the air and the earth

The law of the slot earth_properties gives the resistivity, the relative permittivity and the relative permeability of each earth layer at each frequency. The air keeps its declared values. The model of the slot equivalent_earth then reduces the described layers below the air to the one earth that the earth-return formulas read. The default law, :constant, gives the static values of each layer at every frequency, and the nine soil laws of EarthProperties give values that vary with it. The default model, :bottommost, takes the deepest layer, and :martins2020 and :xue2021 reduce all the layers to one equivalent layer.

Earth-return formulas

LineCableModels.Engine.EarthReturn — Module
LineCableModels.Engine.EarthReturn

Compute the earth-return impedances and potential coefficients of cables. A formula gives the impedance, the potential coefficients, or both, by the methods it defines. It treats the pairs of conductors it declares by their layers: input.earth_layer gives 1 for the air and k for the (k - 1)-th earth layer.

Formulas

Formula{:martins2026}

Earth-return impedances and potential coefficients of conductors in two homogeneous half-spaces, the air, medium 1 with $y > 0$, and the earth, medium 2 with $y < 0$, for fields with the dependence $e^{-\Gamma \ell}$ along the line, where $\ell$ is the axial coordinate. Each cable enters as a solid conductor bounded by its exterior circumference, which lies wholly in one medium. The field of each conductor is that of one axial line source, and each conductor takes the circumferential mean of the field on its circumference, the zeroth azimuthal harmonic. The formula contains the solutions of the underground case [4] and of the mixed overhead and underground case [5]. Its form follows [13].

For each medium $t$, the complex conductivity $\widetilde\sigma_t$ [S/m], the permeability $\mu_t$ [H/m], the transverse propagation parameter $\kappa_t$ [1/m] and the vertical decay parameter $a_t$ [1/m] are

\[\widetilde\sigma_t = \frac{1}{\rho_t} + s \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{r,t}, \qquad \mu_t = \mu_0 \mu_{r,t}, \qquad \kappa_t^2 = s \mu_t \widetilde\sigma_t - \Gamma^2, \qquad a_t = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + \kappa_t^2},\]

where $s = \mathrm{j} 2 \pi f$ is the complex frequency [1/s], $\rho_t$ is the resistivity [Ω·m], $\varepsilon_{r,t}$ and $\mu_{r,t}$ are the relative permittivity and permeability [dimensionless], $\Gamma$ is the longitudinal wavenumber [1/m], and $\lambda$ is the transverse Fourier variable [1/m]. The constants $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$ are ε₀ and μ₀. The square roots take the decaying branch. The spectral denominators are

\[\Delta_\mu = \mu_2 a_1 + \mu_1 a_2, \qquad \Delta_\sigma = \widetilde\sigma_2 a_1 + \widetilde\sigma_1 a_2.\]

Conductor $i$ has its center at $(x_i, y_i)$, the distance $h_i = |y_i|$ to the interface, the exterior radius $r_i$ [m] and the medium $t = m(i)$. In a coefficient $ij$, conductor $i$ is the target and conductor $j$ the source, in the medium $s = m(j)$, and $\bar s$ is the other medium. The distance between the centers is $D_{ij}$, the distance from conductor $i$ to the image of conductor $j$ is $D'_{ij} = \sqrt{x_{ij}^2 + (h_i + h_j)^2}$, with $D'_{ii} = 2 h_i$, and $x_{ij} = x_i - x_j$ [m].

For conductors in the same medium, $t = s$, the means of the direct and image fields and the interface corrections are

\[\begin{aligned} \Lambda_{ii} &= K_0(\kappa_s r_i) - I_0(\kappa_s r_i) K_0(2 \kappa_s h_i), \qquad \Lambda_{ij} = I_0(\kappa_s r_i) \left[K_0(\kappa_s D_{ij}) - K_0(\kappa_s D'_{ij})\right], \\ J_{ij} &= \int_0^\infty \frac{\mu_{\bar s}}{\Delta_\mu} e^{-a_s (h_i + h_j)} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \qquad M_{ij} = \int_0^\infty \frac{\widetilde\sigma_s (\mu_s a_s + \mu_{\bar s} a_{\bar s})}{\Delta_\mu \Delta_\sigma} e^{-a_s (h_i + h_j)} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \end{aligned}\]

where $I_\nu$ and $K_\nu$ are the modified Bessel functions of order $\nu$. The axial vector-potential kernel $\widetilde L_{ij}$ [H/m] and the scalar-potential kernel $\widetilde\Phi_{ij}$ [m/F] are

\[\widetilde L_{ij} = \frac{\mu_s}{2 \pi} \left[\Lambda_{ij} + 2 I_0(\kappa_s r_i) J_{ij}\right], \qquad \widetilde\Phi_{ij} = \frac{s}{2 \pi \widetilde\sigma_s} \left[\Lambda_{ij} + 2 I_0(\kappa_s r_i) M_{ij}\right]\]

for $t = s$, and, from the transmitted fields,

\[\begin{aligned} \widetilde L_{ij} &= \frac{\mu_t \mu_s}{\pi} I_0(\kappa_t r_i) \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-a_t h_i - a_s h_j}}{\Delta_\mu} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \\ \widetilde\Phi_{ij} &= \frac{s}{\pi} I_0(\kappa_t r_i) \int_0^\infty \frac{\mu_1 a_1 + \mu_2 a_2}{\Delta_\mu \Delta_\sigma} e^{-a_t h_i - a_s h_j} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda \end{aligned}\]

for $t \neq s$. The kernel $\widetilde K_{ij} = s \widetilde L_{ij} - (\Gamma^2 / s) \widetilde\Phi_{ij}$ [Ω/m] is the negative mean axial electric field at conductor $i$ per source amplitude at conductor $j$.

The voltage of a conductor is the circumferential mean of the integral of the vertical electric field from a reference level. A conductor in the earth is referred to infinite depth, and a conductor in the air to the interface below it. The source-normalized voltage coefficients $\widetilde P_{ij}$ [m/F] are

\[\begin{aligned} \widetilde P_{ij} &= \delta_{ts} \frac{s}{2 \pi \widetilde\sigma_2} \Lambda_{ij} + \frac{s}{\pi} I_0(\kappa_2 r_i) \int_0^\infty \frac{a_1}{a_2 \Delta_\sigma} e^{-a_2 h_i - a_s h_j} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, & t &= 2, \\ \widetilde P_{ij} &= \delta_{ts} \frac{s}{2 \pi \widetilde\sigma_1} \Lambda_{ij} + \frac{s}{\pi} \int_0^\infty \frac{a_2}{a_1 \Delta_\sigma} \left[I_0(\kappa_1 r_i) e^{-a_1 h_i} - J_0(\lambda r_i)\right] e^{-a_s h_j} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, & t &= 1, \end{aligned}\]

where $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta and $J_0$ is the Bessel function of order zero, which averages the interface endpoint over the horizontal extent of the circumference. For $s = 1$, the integral of the line $t = 1$ is $-\Theta_{ij} / \widetilde\sigma_1$, with $\Theta_{ij}$ the averaged interface correction of [13]. The kernels evaluate it as one integral for a nominal $|\operatorname{Re} \kappa_1 r_i| < 300$ and as its two endpoint terms above.

The enclosed currents follow from the complete field on the circumferences, with the reflected and mutual fields, through the dimensionless current matrix $\mathbf T$, the key T of the formula's block, and the diagonal matrices $\mathbf W$ [dimensionless] and $\mathbf F$ [m/Ω]. Elimination of the source amplitudes gives the earth-return potential coefficients $\mathbf P_g$ [m/F] and impedances $\mathbf Z_g$ [Ω/m]:

\[\mathbf T = \mathbf W^{-1} - \mathbf F \widetilde{\mathbf K}, \qquad W_{ii} = I_0(\kappa_t r_i), \qquad F_{ii} = \frac{2 \pi \widetilde\sigma_t r_i I_1(\kappa_t r_i)}{\kappa_t I_0(\kappa_t r_i)},\]

\[\mathbf P_g = \widetilde{\mathbf P} \mathbf T^{-1}, \qquad \mathbf Z_g = \left(\widetilde{\mathbf K} + \frac{\Gamma^2}{s} \widetilde{\mathbf P}\right) \mathbf T^{-1}.\]

The internal impedances of the conductors are not part of $\mathbf Z_g$: the impedance inside each cable, Zin, gives them.

The integrals run along the complex path that each SpectralIntegral chooses, and integrate evaluates them. The kernel $\widetilde\Phi_{ij}$ enters only for $\Gamma \neq 0$. With $\xi = \kappa_t r_i$, the ratio $I_1(\xi) / (\xi I_0(\xi))$ in $F_{ii}$ is the series $1/2 - \xi^2/16 + \xi^4/96 - 11 \xi^6/6144$ for a nominal $|\xi| < 10^{-3}$. The direct and image terms are evaluated together for a nominal $|\kappa_s D'_{ij}| < 0.5$, and their difference is $\ln(D'_{ij} / D_{ij})$ for $\kappa_s = 0$, with $D_{ii} = r_i$. The implementation scales the column of each source $j$ by $e^{|\operatorname{Re} \kappa_s r_j|}$ with exponentially scaled Bessel functions, and the scaling cancels in $\mathbf P_g$ and $\mathbf Z_g$.

Callables

The kernels are the integrands of the integrals of a pair in $\lambda$, with the states medium and pair that to_buffer! builds:

  • formula(Val(:J), λ, medium, pair): the magnetic coupling.
  • formula(Val(:M), λ, medium, pair): the scalar-potential correction.
  • formula(Val(:P̃), λ, medium, pair): the voltage correction.
  • formula(Val(:Θ), λ, medium, pair): the averaged interface correction of a target in the air.
  • formula(Val(:Θ), Val(:interface), λ, medium, pair): the interface endpoint of that correction.

The entries read the block of the formula, with i the target and j the source:

  • formula(Val(:Z), Val(:self), i, buffers, input, media, s, k) and formula(Val(:Z), Val(:mutual), i, j, buffers, input, media, s, k): an entry of $\mathbf Z_g$ [Ω/m].
  • formula(Val(:P), Val(:self), i, buffers, input, media, s, k) and formula(Val(:P), Val(:mutual), i, j, buffers, input, media, s, k): an entry of $\mathbf P_g$ [m/F].

Formula{:ideal}

Potential coefficients of conductors over an earth of infinite conductivity, from the images of the sources, for conductors in the air:

\[P_{g,ii} = \frac{\ln(2 h_i / r_i)}{2 \pi \varepsilon_1}, \qquad P_{g,ij} = \frac{\ln(D'_{ij} / D_{ij})}{2 \pi \varepsilon_1},\]

where $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{r,1}$ is the permittivity of the air [F/m]. These are $\Lambda_{ii}$ and $\Lambda_{ij}$ at $\kappa = 0$. The earth shields a buried conductor, so each coefficient of a buried or mixed pair is zero. The formula gives the potential coefficients alone.

Formula{:gary1976}

Impedance of conductors in the air over a nonmagnetic earth, from an image plane at the complex depth $p$ [m] [14, 15]:

\[\begin{aligned} p &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{s \mu_0 \sigma_2}}, \\ Z_{g,ii} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[\ln\frac{2 h_i}{r_i} + \ln\frac{h_i + p}{h_i}\right], \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[\ln\frac{D'_{ij}}{D_{ij}} + \ln\frac{\sqrt{y_{ij}^2 + (H_{ij} + 2 p)^2}}{D'_{ij}}\right], \end{aligned}\]

where $\sigma_2 = 1 / \rho_2$ [S/m] is the conductivity of the earth. The lengths [m] are the height $h_i$, the exterior radius $r_i$, the horizontal distance $y_{ij}$ and $H_{ij} = h_i + h_j$. The first logarithm of each entry is $\Lambda_{ii}$ or $\Lambda_{ij}$ at $\kappa = 0$. The formula leaves out the displacement current of the earth, and it gives the impedance alone. A computation refuses a magnetic earth layer.

Formula{:wedepohl1973}

Impedance of buried conductors by the low-order expansion of Wedepohl and Wilcox [3]. The earth has the permeability $\mu_2 = \mu_0 \mu_{r,2}$, with $m = \sqrt{s \mu_2 / \rho_2}$ [1/m], and $e_c$ is the exponential of Euler's constant:

\[\begin{aligned} Z_{g,ii} &= \frac{s \mu_2}{2 \pi} \left[-\ln\frac{e_c m r_i}{2} + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{4 m h_i}{3}\right], \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_2}{2 \pi} \left[-\ln\frac{e_c m D_{ij}}{2} + \frac{1}{2} - \frac{2 m H_{ij}}{3}\right], \end{aligned}\]

where $h_i$ is the depth [m]. The formula leaves out the displacement current of the earth, and it gives the impedance alone. The source bounds the entries by $|m r_i| < 0.25$ and $|m D_{ij}| < 0.25$. A computation warns when the highest frequency of its scan leaves that bound, with $m$ from the static resistivity and permeability of the deepest layer.

Formula{:saad1996}

Impedance of buried conductors in a nonmagnetic earth by the closed form of Saad, Gaba and Giroux [16], with $m = \sqrt{s \mu_0 / \rho_2}$ [1/m]:

\[\begin{aligned} Z_{g,ii} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[K_0(m r_i) + \frac{2 e^{-2 m h_i}}{4 + m^2 r_i^2}\right], \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[K_0(m D_{ij}) + \frac{2 e^{-m H_{ij}}}{4 + m^2 y_{ij}^2}\right], \end{aligned}\]

where $K_0$ is the modified Bessel function of the second kind and order zero. The source proves the form for $y_{ij} / H_{ij} < 1$. The formula leaves out the displacement current of the earth, and it gives the impedance alone. A computation refuses a magnetic earth layer.

Formula{:lucca1994}

Mutual impedance between a conductor in the air and a conductor in a nonmagnetic earth, by the approximation of Lucca [17]. Equation (28) of [18] prints the expression. With the complex depth $p = 1 / \sqrt{s \mu_0 \sigma_2}$ [m]:

\[\begin{aligned} V &= h_a + h_g, \qquad H_c = V + 2 p, \qquad S = \sqrt{H_c^2 + y_{ij}^2}, \qquad D = \sqrt{V^2 + y_{ij}^2}, \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[\ln\frac{S}{D} - \frac{2}{3} \left(\frac{p}{S^2}\right)^3 H_c \left(H_c^2 - 3 y_{ij}^2\right)\right], \end{aligned}\]

where $h_a$ is the height of the conductor in the air and $h_g$ the depth of the conductor in the earth [m]. The horizontal distance is $y_{ij}$ [m], and $\sigma_2 = 1 / \rho_2$ [S/m]. The formula leaves out the displacement current of the earth. It gives the entries of a mixed pair alone, so other formulas fill the air and earth places. A computation refuses a magnetic earth layer.

Formula{:ametani2009}

Mutual impedance between a conductor in the air and a conductor in a nonmagnetic earth, by equation (27) of Ametani, Yoneda, Baba and Nagaoka [18]. With $m = \sqrt{s \mu_0 / \rho_2}$ [1/m] and the complex depth $p = 1 / m$ [m]:

\[\begin{aligned} S &= \sqrt{(h_a + h_g + 2 p)^2 + y_{ij}^2}, \qquad D = \sqrt{(h_a + h_g)^2 + y_{ij}^2}, \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} e^{-h_g / p} \ln\frac{S}{D}, \end{aligned}\]

where $h_a$, $h_g$ and $y_{ij}$ are the lengths of Formula{:lucca1994}. The exponential takes $h_g$ as a positive depth. The formula leaves out the displacement current of the earth. It gives the entries of a mixed pair alone, so other formulas fill the air and earth places. A computation refuses a magnetic earth layer.

Formula{:carson1926}

Impedance of conductors in the air over a nonmagnetic earth, by the correction integral of Carson [19], with $\gamma^2 = s \mu_0 \sigma_2$ [1/m²]:

\[\begin{aligned} J_{ij} &= \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-H_{ij} \lambda} \cos(y_{ij} \lambda)} {\lambda + \sqrt{\lambda^2 + \gamma^2}} \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \\ Z_{g,ii} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[\ln\frac{2 h_i}{r_i} + 2 J_{ii}\right], \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[\ln\frac{D'_{ij}}{D_{ij}} + 2 J_{ij}\right], \end{aligned}\]

where $\sigma_2 = 1 / \rho_2$ [S/m], $J_{ij}$ is in [dimensionless], and the lengths are those of Formula{:gary1976}. The first logarithm of each entry is $\Lambda_{ii}$ or $\Lambda_{ij}$ at $\kappa = 0$. The integral of the source is $\mathrm{j} J_{ij} = P + \mathrm{j} Q$, at $p = H_{ij} \sqrt{\omega \mu_0 \sigma_2}$ and $q = y_{ij} \sqrt{\omega \mu_0 \sigma_2}$. Its series evaluate that integral, and this formula evaluates it on the path of SpectralIntegral, under the options integral of integrate. The formula leaves out the displacement currents of the air and the earth, and it gives the impedance alone. A computation refuses a magnetic earth layer.

Formula{:pollaczek1926}

Impedance of conductors in a nonmagnetic earth and of a pair across the interface, by the Green functions of Pollaczek [20]. The symbols are $\gamma^2 = s \mu_0 \sigma_2$ [1/m²] and $a = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + \gamma^2}$ [1/m]. For conductors in the earth, his equation (20b) gives:

\[\begin{aligned} J_{ij} &= \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-a H_{ij}} \cos(y_{ij} \lambda)}{\lambda + a} \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \\ Z_{g,ii} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[K_0(\gamma r_i) - K_0(2 \gamma h_i) + 2 J_{ii}\right], \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_0}{2 \pi} \left[K_0(\gamma D_{ij}) - K_0(\gamma D'_{ij}) + 2 J_{ij}\right]. \end{aligned}\]

For a pair across the interface, his equations (19a) and (20a) are one expression, since (9) makes the field symmetric in the two points:

\[Z_{g,ij} = \frac{s \mu_0}{\pi} \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-h_a \lambda - h_g a} \cos(y_{ij} \lambda)}{\lambda + a} \, \mathrm{d}\lambda,\]

where $\sigma_2 = 1 / \rho_2$ [S/m] and $J_{ij}$ is in [dimensionless]. The lengths are those of Formula{:gary1976}, with $h_i$ the depth of the conductor, and $h_a$ and $h_g$ those of Formula{:lucca1994}. The formula evaluates the integrals on the path of SpectralIntegral, under the options integral of integrate. Each cable enters as a filament of its exterior radius. The formula leaves out the displacement currents of the air and the earth, and it gives the impedance alone. A computation refuses a magnetic earth layer.

Formula{:papadopoulos2010}

Impedance and potential coefficients of conductors in the earth, by Papadopoulos and coauthors [21], under the longitudinal constant of the earth, $k_x^2 = \omega^2 \mu_2 \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{r,2}$ [1/m²]. The symbols are $\gamma_t^2 = s \mu_t \tilde\sigma_t$ and $\kappa_t^2 = \gamma_t^2 + k_x^2$ [1/m²], and $a_t = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + \kappa_t^2}$ [1/m], the air $t = 1$ and the earth $t = 2$. Their equations (5) and (6) give:

\[\begin{aligned} \Delta &= a_2 \mu_0 + a_1 \mu_2, \\ J_{ij} &= \int_0^\infty \frac{\mu_0}{\Delta} e^{-a_2 H_{ij}} \cos(y_{ij} \lambda) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \\ \tilde P_{ij} &= \int_0^\infty \frac{\mu_0}{\Delta} \left[1 + \frac{\mu_2 a_2 (\gamma_2^2 - \gamma_1^2)}{a_2 \gamma_1^2 \mu_2 + a_1 \gamma_2^2 \mu_0}\right] e^{-a_2 H_{ij}} \cos(y_{ij} \lambda) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \\ \Lambda_{ij} &= K_0(\kappa_2 D_{ij}) - K_0(\kappa_2 D'_{ij}), \\ Z_{g,ij} &= \frac{s \mu_2}{2 \pi} (\Lambda_{ij} + 2 J_{ij}), \qquad P_{g,ij} = \frac{s}{2 \pi \tilde\sigma_2} (\Lambda_{ij} + 2 \tilde P_{ij}), \end{aligned}\]

where $\tilde\sigma_1 = s \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{r,1}$ and $\tilde\sigma_2 = 1 / \rho_2 + s \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{r,2}$ [S/m], $\mu_1 = \mu_0$ and $\mu_2 = \mu_0 \mu_{r,2}$ [H/m], and $J_{ij}$ and $\tilde P_{ij}$ are in [dimensionless]. The lengths are those of Formula{:gary1976}, with $h_i$ the depth of the conductor. Evaluate an integral along its path by adaptive Gauss–Kronrod quadrature with QuadGK. Substitute $\lambda = \zeta u$ for $u \geq 0$ and transform $u$ into $t$:

\[\int_0^{\infty} g(\zeta u)\,\zeta\,\mathrm{d}u = \int_0^1 \zeta\, g\!\left(\frac{\zeta t}{1-t}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{(1-t)^2}.\]

Here $g$ is the integrand and $\zeta$ the path. Map each breakpoint $u_b$ to $t_b = u_b / (1 + u_b)$ in $[0, 1)$ and seed one segment between adjacent points. The error norm is the magnitude of the nominal value.

  • section: controls rtol, atol, and maxevals from defaults.
  • buffers: computation arrays. The points of the integral block are the breakpoints in the coordinate $u$, finite and nonnegative.

Returns

  • The integral value, in the unit of the integrand times its coordinate.
  • The estimated absolute error, in the unit of the integral value.

Notes

An estimated error above max(atol, rtol * abs(nominal(value))), or a nonfinite estimate, produces a warning. The method still returns the value.

Errors

LineCableModels.Engine.EarthProperties

Compute the resistivity, relative permittivity, and relative permeability of the earth layers, and their equivalent earth.

A soil law reads the resistivity $\rho_\mathrm{low}$ [Ω·m] of a layer, its conductivity $\sigma_\mathrm{low} = 1 / \rho_\mathrm{low}$ [S/m] and the frequency $f$ [Hz]. It gives the resistivity $\rho(f)$ [Ω·m], which is $1 / \sigma(f)$ unless the law states otherwise, and the relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r(f)$ [dimensionless]. The described layer keeps the relative permeability and the thickness of layer. The constant $\varepsilon_0$ is ε₀.

Formulas

Formula{:constant}

Properties of an earth layer independent of the frequency $f$:

\[\rho(f) = \rho, \qquad \varepsilon_r(f) = \varepsilon_r, \qquad \mu_r(f) = \mu_r,\]

where $\rho$ is the resistivity of the layer [Ω·m], and $\varepsilon_r$ and $\mu_r$ are its relative permittivity and permeability [dimensionless].

Callable

formula(layer, f) returns layer, whose properties hold at every frequency.

  • layer: earth layer.
  • f: frequency [Hz].

Formula{:bottommost}

Equivalent earth of the layers below the air: the deepest described layer, the identity for one layer.

Callable

formula(layers, f) returns the last of layers.

  • layers: the layers below the air, as the law describes them at f.

Formula{:alipio2014}

and $\rho(f) = 1000 / \sigma(f)$. The options are exponent $\gamma$, epsilon_high $\varepsilon_\mathrm{high}$, scale $a$ and sigma_exponent $b$. A computation warns outside 100 Hz to 4 MHz.

Formula{:martins2020}

Equivalent conductivity of Martins-Britto, Lopes and Rondineau [6]. From $\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},N} = \sigma_N$ at the deepest layer, the recursion climbs to the layer below the air, where $\sigma_\mathrm{eq} = \sigma_{\mathrm{eq},1}$:

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},k} = \sigma_k \left[ \frac{\sqrt{\sigma_k} + \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}} - \left(\sqrt{\sigma_k} - \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}}\right) e^{-2 h_k \sqrt{\pi f \mu_k \sigma_k}}} {\sqrt{\sigma_k} + \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}} + \left(\sqrt{\sigma_k} - \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}}\right) e^{-2 h_k \sqrt{\pi f \mu_k \sigma_k}}} \right]^2,\]

where layer $k$ has the conductivity $\sigma_k = 1 / \rho_k$ [S/m], the thickness $h_k$ [m] and the permeability $\mu_k = \mu_0 \mu_{r,k}$ [H/m], and $\mu_0$ is μ₀. The layer it returns has the resistivity $1 / \sigma_\mathrm{eq}$ and takes the relative permittivity and the relative permeability of the deepest layer. The source validates the equivalent on overhead conductors from 1 Hz to 2 MHz.

Formula{:xue2021}

Equivalent propagation constant of Xue and coauthors for a nonmagnetic earth [7]. With $s = \mathrm{j} \omega$, $\omega = 2 \pi f$ and the constants $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$, layer $k$ of conductivity $\sigma_k$ [S/m], relative permittivity $\varepsilon_{r,k}$ and thickness $h_k$ [m] has the constant $\gamma_k$ [1/m] relative to the air. From $\gamma_{\mathrm{eq},N} = \gamma_N$ at the deepest layer, the recursion climbs to the layer below the air, where $\gamma_\mathrm{eq} = \gamma_{\mathrm{eq},1}$:

\[\begin{aligned} \gamma_k^2 &= s \mu_0 \left(\sigma_k + s \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{r,k}\right) - s^2 \mu_0 \varepsilon_0, \\ \gamma_{\mathrm{eq},k} &= \gamma_k \frac{\gamma_k + \gamma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1} - \left(\gamma_k - \gamma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}\right) e^{-2 h_k \gamma_k}} {\gamma_k + \gamma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1} + \left(\gamma_k - \gamma_{\mathrm{eq},k+1}\right) e^{-2 h_k \gamma_k}}. \end{aligned}\]

The layer it returns parametrizes $\gamma_\mathrm{eq}^2$ by its resistivity $1 / \sigma_\mathrm{eq}$ and its relative permittivity:

\[\sigma_\mathrm{eq} = \frac{\Im\left(\gamma_\mathrm{eq}^2\right)}{\omega \mu_0}, \qquad \varepsilon_{r,\mathrm{eq}} = 1 - \frac{\Re\left(\gamma_\mathrm{eq}^2\right)}{\omega^2 \mu_0 \varepsilon_0}.\]

This $\varepsilon_{r,\mathrm{eq}}$ is negative where the layers are inductive. The layer takes the relative permeability of the deepest layer. A computation refuses an earth layer whose relative permeability differs from 1. The source reports errors below 4 % from 1 Hz to 5 MHz on overhead conductors.
