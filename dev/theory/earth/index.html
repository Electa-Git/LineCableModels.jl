Earth return

The physical model

The air above the interface y = 0 and the earth below it are homogeneous half-spaces, each with its resistivity $\rho$, relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$ and relative permeability $\mu_r$. Each cable is a source of circumferential currents on its exterior circle, which lies wholly in one medium. The formula closes the circumference currents of all cables completely through the matrix L of its state, and the fields vary along the line as $e^{-\Gamma x}$, with a prescribed longitudinal wavenumber $\Gamma$, zero by default.

The formula gives two quantities per pair of cables, from their exterior surfaces: the earth-return impedance Zg and the earth-return potential coefficients Pg. They are the matrices $\mathbf Z_{\mathrm{env}}$ and $\mathbf P_{\mathrm{env}}$ of the matrix formulation, added to every pair of conductors of each pair of cables.

The fields of each medium are spectral integrals over the real line of the transverse wavenumber. The formula evaluates them on a complex ray, which ray! chooses with the breakpoints of the quadrature, and integrate evaluates each integral by adaptive quadrature. The option integral of the formula selects the method and its controls, integral = (method = :quad, rtol, atol, maxevals).

Validity of the default formula

The default formula, :unified, holds within two ranges.

  • Prescribed Γ. A nonzero Γ lies between the propagation constants of the two media. It is in range at a frequency where Im γair ≤ Im Γ ≤ Im γearth and Re Γ ≤ Re γ_earth. Γ = 0, the default, always lies in range.
  • Thick receiver. The formula represents each receiving conductor by the mean field on its exterior circumference. A conductor that is thick compared with the transverse wavelength of its medium receives a voltage that departs from the full-field value. The error grows as (κm rp)², where κm is the outgoing root of γm² − Γ² in the medium of conductor p and rp is its exterior radius, the jacket for an insulated cable. The range is |κm r_p| ≤ 0.1. Emission from a thick conductor is accurate.

The properties of the air and the earth

The law of the slot earth_properties gives the resistivity, the relative permittivity and the relative permeability of the air and of the earth at each frequency. The one law of this release, :constant, gives the static values of each layer at every frequency.

Earth-return formulas

LineCableModels.Engine.EarthReturn — Module
LineCableModels.Engine.EarthReturn

Compute the earth-return impedances and potential coefficients of cables. A formula gives the impedance, the potential coefficients, or both, by the methods it defines.

Formulas

Formula{:unified}

Earth-return impedances and potential coefficients of conductors in two homogeneous half-spaces, the air, medium 1 with $y > 0$, and the earth, medium 2 with $y < 0$, for fields with the dependence $e^{-\Gamma \ell}$ along the line, where $\ell$ is the axial coordinate. Each cable enters as a solid conductor bounded by its exterior circumference, which lies wholly in one medium. The field of each conductor is that of one axial line source, and each conductor takes the circumferential mean of the field on its circumference, the zeroth azimuthal harmonic. The formula contains the solutions of the underground case [3] and of the mixed overhead and underground case [4].

For each medium $t$, the complex conductivity $\widehat\sigma_t$ [S/m], the permeability $\mu_t$ [H/m], the transverse propagation parameter $\kappa_t$ [1/m] and the vertical decay parameter $a_t$ [1/m] are

\[\widehat\sigma_t = \frac{1}{\rho_t} + s \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_{r,t}, \qquad \mu_t = \mu_0 \mu_{r,t}, \qquad \kappa_t^2 = s \mu_t \widehat\sigma_t - \Gamma^2, \qquad a_t = \sqrt{\lambda^2 + \kappa_t^2},\]

where $s = \mathrm{j} 2 \pi f$ is the complex frequency [1/s], $\rho_t$ is the resistivity [Ω·m], $\varepsilon_{r,t}$ and $\mu_{r,t}$ are the relative permittivity and permeability [dimensionless], $\Gamma$ is the longitudinal wavenumber [1/m], and $\lambda$ is the transverse Fourier variable [1/m]. The constants $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$ are vacuum_permittivity and vacuum_permeability. The square roots take the decaying branch. The spectral denominators are

\[\Delta_\mu = \mu_2 a_1 + \mu_1 a_2, \qquad \Delta_\sigma = \widehat\sigma_2 a_1 + \widehat\sigma_1 a_2.\]

Conductor $i$ has its center at $(x_i, y_i)$, the distance $h_i = |y_i|$ to the interface, the exterior radius $r_i$ [m] and the medium $t = m(i)$. In a coefficient $ij$, conductor $i$ is the target and conductor $j$ the source, in the medium $s = m(j)$, and $\bar s$ is the other medium. The distance between the centers is $D_{ij}$, the distance from conductor $i$ to the image of conductor $j$ is $D'_{ij} = \sqrt{x_{ij}^2 + (h_i + h_j)^2}$, with $D'_{ii} = 2 h_i$, and $x_{ij} = x_i - x_j$ [m].

For conductors in the same medium, $t = s$, the means of the direct and image fields and the interface corrections are

\[\begin{aligned} \Lambda_{ii} &= K_0(\kappa_s r_i) - I_0(\kappa_s r_i) K_0(2 \kappa_s h_i), \qquad \Lambda_{ij} = I_0(\kappa_s r_i) \left[K_0(\kappa_s D_{ij}) - K_0(\kappa_s D'_{ij})\right], \\ J_{ij} &= \int_0^\infty \frac{\mu_{\bar s}}{\Delta_\mu} e^{-a_s (h_i + h_j)} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \qquad M_{ij} = \int_0^\infty \frac{\widehat\sigma_s (\mu_s a_s + \mu_{\bar s} a_{\bar s})}{\Delta_\mu \Delta_\sigma} e^{-a_s (h_i + h_j)} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \end{aligned}\]

where $I_\nu$ and $K_\nu$ are the modified Bessel functions of order $\nu$. The axial vector-potential kernel $\widetilde L_{ij}$ [H/m] and the scalar-potential kernel $\widetilde\Phi_{ij}$ [m/F] are

\[\widetilde L_{ij} = \frac{\mu_s}{2 \pi} \left[\Lambda_{ij} + 2 I_0(\kappa_s r_i) J_{ij}\right], \qquad \widetilde\Phi_{ij} = \frac{s}{2 \pi \widehat\sigma_s} \left[\Lambda_{ij} + 2 I_0(\kappa_s r_i) M_{ij}\right]\]

for $t = s$, and, from the transmitted fields,

\[\begin{aligned} \widetilde L_{ij} &= \frac{\mu_t \mu_s}{\pi} I_0(\kappa_t r_i) \int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-a_t h_i - a_s h_j}}{\Delta_\mu} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, \\ \widetilde\Phi_{ij} &= \frac{s}{\pi} I_0(\kappa_t r_i) \int_0^\infty \frac{\mu_1 a_1 + \mu_2 a_2}{\Delta_\mu \Delta_\sigma} e^{-a_t h_i - a_s h_j} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda \end{aligned}\]

for $t \neq s$. The kernel $\widetilde K_{ij} = s \widetilde L_{ij} - (\Gamma^2 / s) \widetilde\Phi_{ij}$ [Ω/m] is the negative mean axial electric field at conductor $i$ per source amplitude at conductor $j$.

The voltage of a conductor is the circumferential mean of the integral of the vertical electric field from a reference level. A conductor in the earth is referred to infinite depth, and a conductor in the air to the interface below it. The source-normalized voltage coefficients $\widetilde P_{ij}$ [m/F] are

\[\begin{aligned} \widetilde P_{ij} &= \delta_{ts} \frac{s}{2 \pi \widehat\sigma_2} \Lambda_{ij} + \frac{s}{\pi} I_0(\kappa_2 r_i) \int_0^\infty \frac{a_1}{a_2 \Delta_\sigma} e^{-a_2 h_i - a_s h_j} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, & t &= 2, \\ \widetilde P_{ij} &= \delta_{ts} \frac{s}{2 \pi \widehat\sigma_1} \Lambda_{ij} + \frac{s}{\pi} \int_0^\infty \frac{a_2}{a_1 \Delta_\sigma} \left[I_0(\kappa_1 r_i) e^{-a_1 h_i} - J_0(\lambda r_i)\right] e^{-a_s h_j} \cos(\lambda x_{ij}) \, \mathrm{d}\lambda, & t &= 1, \end{aligned}\]

where $\delta$ is the Kronecker delta and $J_0$ is the Bessel function of order zero, which averages the interface endpoint over the horizontal extent of the circumference.

The enclosed currents follow from the complete field on the circumferences, with the reflected and mutual fields, through the dimensionless current matrix $\mathbf T$, held as L in the state of the formula, and the diagonal matrices $\mathbf W$ [dimensionless] and $\mathbf F$ [m/Ω]. Elimination of the source amplitudes gives the earth-return potential coefficients $\mathbf P_g$ [m/F] and impedances $\mathbf Z_g$ [Ω/m]:

\[\mathbf T = \mathbf W^{-1} - \mathbf F \widetilde{\mathbf K}, \qquad W_{ii} = I_0(\kappa_t r_i), \qquad F_{ii} = \frac{2 \pi \widehat\sigma_t r_i I_1(\kappa_t r_i)}{\kappa_t I_0(\kappa_t r_i)},\]

\[\mathbf P_g = \widetilde{\mathbf P} \mathbf T^{-1}, \qquad \mathbf Z_g = \left(\widetilde{\mathbf K} + \frac{\Gamma^2}{s} \widetilde{\mathbf P}\right) \mathbf T^{-1}.\]

The internal impedances of the conductors are not part of $\mathbf Z_g$: the impedance inside each cable, Zin, gives them.

The integrals run along the complex ray that ray! selects, and integrate evaluates them. The kernel $\widetilde\Phi_{ij}$ enters only for $\Gamma \neq 0$. With $\xi = \kappa_t r_i$, the ratio $I_1(\xi) / (\xi I_0(\xi))$ in $F_{ii}$ is the series $1/2 - \xi^2/16 + \xi^4/96 - 11 \xi^6/6144$ for a nominal $|\xi| < 10^{-3}$. The direct and image terms are evaluated together for a nominal $|\kappa_s D'_{ij}| < 0.5$, and their difference is $\ln(D'_{ij} / D_{ij})$ for $\kappa_s = 0$, with $D_{ii} = r_i$. The implementation scales the column of each source $j$ by $e^{|\operatorname{Re} \kappa_s r_j|}$ with exponentially scaled Bessel functions, and the scaling cancels in $\mathbf P_g$ and $\mathbf Z_g$.

LineCableModels.Engine.integrate — Function
integrate(
    integral::LineCableModels.Engine.SpectralIntegral,
    ::Val{:quad},
    section::NamedTuple,
    buffers;
    points
)

Evaluate an integral by adaptive Gauss–Kronrod quadrature with QuadGK. Transform its nonnegative coordinate $\lambda$ into $t$:

\[\int_0^\infty g(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\lambda = \int_0^1 g\!\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{(1-t)^2}.\]

Here $g$ is the complete integrand. Map each breakpoint $\lambda_b$ to $t_b = \lambda_b / (1 + \lambda_b)$ in $[0, 1)$ and seed one segment between adjacent points. The error norm is the magnitude of the nominal value.

Arguments

  • integral: complete integrand and its integral.
  • Val(:quad): adaptive quadrature method tag.
  • section: controls rtol, atol, and maxevals from defaults.
  • buffers: computation arrays with the integral block.

Keywords

  • points: finite, nonnegative breakpoints in the coordinate of the integrand.

Returns

  • The integral value, in the unit of the integrand times its coordinate.
  • The estimated absolute error, in the unit of the integral value.

Notes

An estimated error above max(atol, rtol * abs(nominal(value))), or a nonfinite estimate, produces a warning. The method still returns the value.

Errors

  • Throws DomainError for a negative or nonfinite breakpoint, or a nonfinite integral value.

LineCableModels.Engine.ray! — Function
ray!(
    buffers,
    wavenumbers::Tuple,
    poles::Tuple,
    scales::Tuple,
    height,
    reach
) -> Any

Choose a complex spectral ray and write quadrature breakpoints in its dimensionless coordinate $t$. The spectral coordinate is

\[\lambda = R e^{\mathrm{j}\theta}t, \qquad R = \max\!\left(\max_m |k_m|,\frac{1}{h}\right), \qquad h\cos\theta-y\sin\theta > 0.\]

Here $h$ is height, $y$ is reach, and $k_m$ are medium wavenumbers. The angle is

\[\theta = \min\left(\frac{\pi}{6}, \arctan\frac{h}{2 \max(y, \epsilon)}, \min_{\zeta} \frac{\arg \zeta}{2}\right),\]

where $\epsilon$ is the machine epsilon of the coordinate type and $\zeta$ runs over the branch points $\pm\mathrm{j}k_m$ and the poles in the open first quadrant. These choices make the geometric weight $e^{-h\lambda}\cos(y\lambda)$ decay along the ray.

Use nominal values for all decisions. The scales are nonzero wavenumber magnitudes, finite positive formula scales, and the reciprocal geometric decay. Each scale contributes half, once, and twice its value. Each singularity contributes up to five positive points around its projection. Bridge gaps by factors of four, divide points by $R$, include zero and one, then sort and remove duplicates.

Arguments

  • buffers: computation arrays with the integral block.
  • wavenumbers: tuple of medium wavenumbers [1/m].
  • poles: tuple of poles of the integrand [1/m]. Ignore zero entries.
  • scales: tuple of formula-specific spectral scales [1/m]. Only finite positive values contribute.
  • height: sum of source and receiver distances from the interface [m].
  • reach: horizontal separation, with any receiver-radius allowance needed by the integrand [m].

Returns

  • The complex ray $R e^{\mathrm{j}\theta}$, in [1/m]. The method also fills buffers.integral.points with dimensionless breakpoints.

Errors

  • Throws DomainError when the geometric weight does not decay along the ray.
