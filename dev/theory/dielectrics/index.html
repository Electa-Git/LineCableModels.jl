Dielectrics

The dielectric layers between a conductor and the next conductor outward form its interval. The magnetic field in the interval adds a series term to the loop impedance of the conductor [1], with the relative permeability mu_r_ins of the interval. For a stranded conductor, that permeability includes the solenoid factor of the helical current of its wires, as Cable data model describes.

Each layer also has an admittivity $\kappa$, which the shunt formula reads to build the potential coefficients inside the cable by the series chain of the layers, which the matrix formulation page gives. An insulator takes the law of insulation_admittance and a semicon the law of semicon_admittance, by the class of its material. The law :lossless, the default of both slots, leaves out conduction and polarization loss. The law :lossy adds them from the fields rho and tan_delta of the material.

Insulation impedance

LineCableModels.Engine.InsulationImpedance — Module
LineCableModels.Engine.InsulationImpedance

Compute the magnetic series-impedance term across an annular dielectric.

Formulas

Formula{:ametani1980}

Magnetic term of the series impedance across an annular dielectric layer, from the field between its radii [1, 10]:

\[z = \frac{s \mu_0 \mu_r}{2 \pi} \ln \frac{r_\mathrm{ex}}{r_\mathrm{in}},\]

where $s$ is the complex frequency [1/s], $r_\mathrm{in}$ and $r_\mathrm{ex}$ are the inner and outer radii of the layer [m], and $\mu_r$ is its relative permeability [dimensionless]. The constant $\mu_0$ is μ₀. The term is zero when $r_\mathrm{in}$ is zero or equals $r_\mathrm{ex}$.

Callable

formula(r_in, r_ex, μ_r, s) returns $z$ [Ω/m].

  • r_in: inner radius [m].
  • r_ex: outer radius, in [m].
  • μ_r: relative permeability [dimensionless].
  • s: complex frequency, j 2π f [1/s].

Insulation admittance

LineCableModels.Engine.InsulationAdmittance — Module
LineCableModels.Engine.InsulationAdmittance

Compute the admittivity of insulator layers.

Formulas

Formula{:lossless}

Admittivity of a dielectric without conduction or polarization loss, the frequency-domain constitutive relation of its displacement current:

\[\kappa = \mathrm{j} 2 \pi f \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r,\]

where $f$ is the frequency [Hz], $\kappa$ is the admittivity [S/m] and $\varepsilon_r$ is the relative permittivity eps_r of the material [dimensionless]. The constant $\varepsilon_0$ is ε₀.

Callable

formula(material, f) returns $\kappa$ [S/m] of an insulator layer.

  • material: material of the layer.
  • f: frequency [Hz].

Formula{:lossy}

Admittivity of a dielectric with conduction, polarization loss and displacement current:

\[\kappa = \frac{1}{\rho} + 2 \pi f \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r \tan\delta + \mathrm{j} 2 \pi f \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r,\]

where $\rho$ is the resistivity rho of the material [Ω·m] and $\tan\delta$ is its loss tangent tan_delta [dimensionless]. The other symbols are as in Formula{:lossless}.

Callable

formula(material, f) returns $\kappa$ [S/m] of an insulator layer.

  • material: material of the layer.
  • f: frequency [Hz].
