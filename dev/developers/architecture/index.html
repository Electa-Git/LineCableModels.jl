Architecture

This page is the architectural specification of LineCableModels. It states the structure that the code implements and that every change preserves. The rules and patterns say how to extend the package within that structure, and the conventions say how the code and its documentation are written.

The package computes the series impedance and the shunt admittance of cable systems per unit length. A problem describes the cables, the earth, the temperature and the frequencies. The formulation selects the formula of each step, and the backend executes the computation.

Three axes

The problem, the formulation and the backend answer three different questions. They are orthogonal.

  • The problem says what is computed. LineParametersProblem combines a cable system, an earth model, the operating temperature and the frequencies of the scan.
  • The formulation says how. It is a methodology: one formula for each step, with the controls of the methodology. CoaxialFormulation is the methodology of concentric tubes. FEM is the methodology of a field solve with the material laws.
  • The backend says who computes. LCMBackend is this package's own implementation, in process. GmshBackend runs the Gmsh and GetDP processes of the extension.

To solve a problem, a backend implements a formulation, a methodology with its own formula for each step. A formulation is the same object on every backend. The result records that same object. A backend implements a formulation by a compute method. It implements each formula of the formulation by the methods of its own route. The trait backend gives the default backend of a formulation type and nothing more.

result = compute(problem, CoaxialFormulation())               # on LCMBackend, the default
result = compute(problem, FEM(); workers = 4)                  # on GmshBackend, after `using Gmsh`
result = compute(LCMBackend(), problem, CoaxialFormulation(); trace = true)

A backend refuses before it runs what it cannot honor: a formula it has no route for, or an option it cannot apply. Results never claim a computation that did not take place.

Controls live at two levels. A formula's numerical controls belong to the formula. A methodology's controls belong to the formulation. A backend's process controls belong to the backend. A control belongs to whoever makes the approximation it controls.

Modules and ownership

The package is six modules in one load order, and two extensions.

ModuleOwns
Commonsthe grammar: validate, resolve, build, compute, defaults, select, the abstract types AbstractFormulation and AbstractBackend, the option records
MaterialsMaterial{T, K}, the properties of a material with its class K as a type parameter
EarthEarthLayer, EarthModel, homogeneous: the air and the earth as a stack of layers
DataModelthe cross-sections, the placement, the declared parts, the cable design, the cable system, the blueprint and the builders of the surface syntax
Enginethe problem, the formulations, the backends, the formula families, the numerical input, the buffers, the loop, the reductions, the spectral tools and the result
CADthe curves and contours of the cross-sections and the verb draw
LineCableModelsGmshExtthe finite-element backend, with its model, geometry, meshes, GetDP scan and result
LineCableModelsMeasurementsExtthe propagation of input uncertainty: methods only

The formula families are the eight child modules of Engine: InternalImpedance, InsulationImpedance, InsulationAdmittance, SemiconAdmittance, EarthReturn, Shunt, Resistivity and EarthProperties.

The structure follows four rules.

  • The root module LineCableModels defines nothing. It includes the modules and imports their names. It exports or declares public the names users see.
  • A module references only the modules before it, its ancestors and its descendants.
  • Each concept has one owner, one name and one implementation. Each name has one meaning.
  • A module imports each foreign name explicitly from its owner. Its owner exports or declares public a name that other modules use.

A method that extends a function of another module mentions a type of its own module. Constructors belong to the owner of their type. Every file of a module lies under the directory of its module file. No core method is defined under ext/.

Grammar

Commons defines the verbs that the other modules extend with methods.

FunctionMeaning
validate(subject, context...)reject an invalid input before its consumer uses it, and return subject unchanged
resolve(context, declaration)give a declared part or layer its geometry in its context, or place a cross-section in a frame
build(Target, declarations...)construct one completed object of Target from its declarations
compute(problem, formulation; kw...)run one computation of a problem by a formulation on the backend that runs it
defaults(owner)the declared option keys and default values of a formula, a formulation or a backend
select(default, value)convert an option to its declared type, or lift a formula selection into its slot

The grammar belongs to Commons however many modules extend it. Any other definition in Commons is user API, or it is used by methods of two owners outside Commons. Each definition of Commons is exported or public and has a docstring, and its name appears in a test under test/commons/.

The options are stored in records of two types. FormulationOptions{O, NT} is the record of a formula or a formulation O. ComputationOptions{B, NT} is the record of a backend B. Each record is built once, at construction, from defaults(O) and the keywords given. An unknown key throws. Each value takes its declared type. validate checks the values. The type of a record does not depend on which keys the caller passed, so one computation compiles one specialization.

Data model

The data model has three layers. It encodes a cable as a declaration and describes the cable as resolved regions with tables. From the description it draws the equivalences that the formulations read. It never rejects a physically valid design for a missing formula. A formula that cannot treat an input throws when it is called.

Cross-sections

A cross-section is one type with the methods of one unary interface: area, centroid, support, distance, boundary and resolve. r_in and r_ex exist on circular cross-sections only. No method takes two cross-sections. The primitives are Circle, Annulus, Rectangle and AnnularSector. Offset(t) is an operator: a layer declared by its thickness, resolved on the outline of the part inside it. DifferenceShape is the cross-section of a fill, a container without the outlines of its contents. A new kind of cross-section is one type and its methods. The structure above it does not change.

Declaration

A cable is a tree of parts. Each node has one job.

NodeJob
Region(tag, primitive, material)one material in one cross-section, named by its tag
Stack(parts...)outward layering: each part resolves against the outline of the part inside it
Terminal(name, part)every conductive region of part belongs to the terminal name
Repeat(pattern, part)copies of part at the placements of a pattern, such as CircularPattern
Lay(helix, repeat)each copy follows a Helix of a LayRatio about the center of the repeat
Enclosure(fill, contents)a fill region, whose primitive is the container, around the contents

The material's class tells whether a region conducts. A conductive region lies in a terminal. A region of an insulator or a semicon belongs to no terminal. A terminal name is an identification and nothing else. The circuit role of a terminal belongs to the connections of the system.

Description

build(CableDesign, id, parts...) resolves the tree into a CableDesign. The design stores the placed regions from the inside out, the outline, and tables of the terminals, the repeats, the lays and the fills. A table has a row only for a fact that exists. The design's number type T is the promotion of the number types of its cross-sections and its materials. An uncertain material widens T. The design validates itself at construction. The regions cover the outline without gaps or overlaps. Every region of an enclosure lies inside its container. Every conductive region belongs to a terminal.

Equivalence

flatten(design) builds a CableBlueprint for the coaxial formulations. Each terminal becomes an equivalent tube. A course of laid wires becomes a tube whose material reproduces the direct-current resistance and the geometric mean radius of the wires. The overlength of the helix enters the resistivity. The solenoid effect of the helix enters the relative permeability of the dielectric interval, mu_r_ins. The dielectric layers between two consecutive conductors form the interval of the inner one. The lay correction is made in the equivalence. The earth-return formulas never see it.

System

build(CableSystem, designs, placements; connections, id, line_length) places cables in one cross-section. connections is one named tuple per cable, keyed by terminal name. An index k ≥ 1 is a live conductor, a repeated index bundles conductors, and 0 eliminates one. Every terminal name must appear. The system rejects overlapping cables and keeps the declared placements. A cable may touch the interface between air and earth and may never cross it. The unified formula and the finite-element model refuse a cable that touches it.

Surface syntax

@cable and @terminal and the builders core, insulation, screen, sheath, bedding, jacket, stranded and armor lower to the nodes above. They do not add a capability.

copper = Material(:conductor, 1.7241e-8, 1, 1, 20, 0.00393)
polyethylene = Material(:insulator, 1.0e14, 2.3)
wire = @cable "insulated_wire" begin
    @terminal :core begin
        core(copper; r = 4.0e-3)
    end
    insulation(polyethylene; t = 1.0e-3)
end

Engine

Formula families

A formula is one object per family: Family.Formula{ID, O}. The identifier ID is a Symbol in the type. The options O are a FormulationOptions{Formula{ID}} record. The family module is the context: EarthReturn.Formula{:unified} and InternalImpedance.Formula{:schelkunoff1934} never meet. Each family exports Formula and FORMULAS, the tuple of its identifiers. The unknown-identifier error prints that tuple.

A new formula is three parts and nothing else. It adds a defaults method for its options, a line of the registry in names.toml mirrored by FORMULAS, and its physics methods. FormulaDefinition, Expression, Functor and a :default identifier do not exist.

Formula methods take the formula object first. The ! marks a mutating method. The destination follows the formula. A formula's per-frequency shared state is its prepare! method, a no-op by default. A formula that needs arrays of its own adds an initialize_buffers method.

FamilyFormulaMethods it defines
InternalImpedance:schelkunoff1934impedance(formula, r_in, r_ex, rho, mu_r, s), the inner, outer and transfer surface impedances
InsulationImpedance:ametani1980impedance(formula, r_in, r_ex, mu_r, s), the magnetic term of an annular dielectric
InsulationAdmittance:losslessadmittivity(formula, material, f) of an insulator
SemiconAdmittance:losslessadmittivity(formula, material, f) of a semicon
EarthReturn:unifiedprepare!, impedance!, potential!, initialize_buffers, validate
Shunt:seriespotential!(formula, Pin, input, kappa, s), the series chain of the layers
Resistivity:linearresistivity(formula, material, temperature)
EarthProperties:constantproperties(formula, layer, f)

Formulation

CoaxialFormulation is a struct of nine typed slots and one options record. A slot accepts :id, :id => (key = value, ...) or a Formula of its family. The keyword constructor lifts each selection once, through select. The options are Γ, the longitudinal wavenumber the computation assumes, and the reductions bundle, kron and transposition.

CoaxialFormulation(; internal_impedance = :schelkunoff1934, insulation_impedance = :ametani1980,
    insulation_admittance = :lossless, semicon_admittance = :lossless,
    earth_impedance = :unified, earth_admittance = :unified, shunt = :series,
    resistivity = :linear, earth_properties = :constant, Γ = complex(0.0),
    bundle = true, kron = true, transposition = false)

Both earth slots are typed by one family, EarthReturn. A formula of that family gives the impedance, the potential coefficients, or both, by the methods it defines. A formula in a slot whose quantity it does not give is refused at construction. If both slots take a formula of one identifier, the two formulas must be equal, because a formula that gives both quantities is one computation. Equal formulas have one type, so formulas(formulation) lists the earth formula once by dispatch, and its prepare! runs once per frequency.

FEM is the formulation of the field solve. It keeps the four law slots that the field solve reads, insulation_admittance, semicon_admittance, resistivity and earth_properties, and the same options. Every control of the field solve belongs to the Gmsh backend.

Backends and dispatch

The backends are singletons of AbstractBackend. compute has four methods in Engine.

compute(problem, formulation; kw...)                       # the default backend of the formulation type
compute(backend, problem, formulation; kw...)              # a chosen backend, the options built once
compute(backend, problem, formulation, options)            # the fallback: "does not implement"
compute(::GmshBackend, problem, formulation; kw...)        # without Gmsh: "run `using Gmsh` first"

LCMBackend computes a CoaxialFormulation in process. The Gmsh extension adds the methods of GmshBackend for FEM. Loading Gmsh replaces an error by a computation without overwriting a method. A later backend adds a singleton, a defaults method, compute methods and refusals. The formulation types stay as they are.

One computation

The scalar compute of LCMBackend depends on nothing above it. A parametric layer, when it arrives, calls it per point, never the reverse. Its steps are fixed.

  1. validate(problem, formulation) and validate(problem, formula) for each formula.
  2. CoaxialInput(problem, options) builds the blueprint of every cable, the placements and the reduction plan from reductions(system, options).
  3. initialize_buffers(formulation, problem, input): every array once, before the loop.
  4. The loop solve!, its own method, over the frequencies.
  5. LineParameters from the filled arrays, validated once.

At each frequency the loop runs these steps in order.

  1. The admittivity of each layer and the properties of the air and the earth.
  2. The series matrix inside each cable, by the loop recursion of the surface impedances.
  3. The potential matrix inside each cable, by the series chain of the layers.
  4. prepare! of each distinct earth formula, then its impedances and potential coefficients.
  5. The earth matrices added to every pair of conductors.
  6. The bundle and Kron reductions.
  7. Y = s P⁻¹ from the reduced potential matrix.
  8. The ideal transposition, when selected.

After the first call, the loop allocates only the pivot vectors of lu!. The test of the loop measures that number exactly.

Buffers and tool levels

initialize_buffers is the one action that builds buffers. It builds the base keys and folds over formulas(formulation), so that a formula adds its own arrays. A base key is a value computed once and read again, or a value one step hands to the next. A value made and used inside one step is inline. One formula's scratch is never a base key.

A name is defined at the level whose every member uses it, and nowhere higher. The engine defines three spectral tools. SpectralIntegral is the complete integrand of a Sommerfeld-type integral. integrate processes it by a method selected with a Val tag. ray! chooses the ray of the integral and the points where the quadrature subdivides. Every spectral earth-return formula uses them. A family defines what every formula of the family declares. Everything else is the formula's, in the bodies of its physics methods. The unified formula defines its kernels, its direct terms and its pair loop inside prepare!, and one private operation, i0m1.

Result

LineParameters{T, F, N} stores Z and Y as arrays indexed by row, column and frequency. It also stores the frequencies, the label of each retained conductor, the line length, the formulation and the trace. The trace N is typed per backend. For LCMBackend it is the stage matrices Zin, Pin, Zg and Pg per frequency, or four empty arrays when the computation did not keep them. A result without a trace stores an empty record, never nothing.

Numbers

The problem promotes every number with Float64, so a Float32 input computes in Float64 and never crashes. A Measurement input keeps its type through the data model and the engine. BigFloat is unsupported: SpecialFunctions has no Bessel methods for it, and the honest error is the MethodError at the first call.

Extensions

Finite-element backend

using Gmsh loads LineCableModelsGmshExt. The extension reads the engine's problem, the laws of FEM and the reduction plan of reductions. From the data model it reads the placed regions and their cross-sections, and from CAD the curves. It defines defaults(::Type{GmshBackend}) and the compute methods of GmshBackend.

Its files follow the computation. options.jl validates the record and defines GetdpBinary and the table of native controls. model.jl builds the field model of a problem: the material groups, the terminals and the medium of each cable. geometry.jl builds the Gmsh geometry through draw and writes physical.geo. mesh.jl writes the model data and meshes every frequency, one Gmsh process at a time per worker. getdp.jl selects the executable and scans the frequencies with GetDP. result.jl reduces the terminal matrices and builds the result. The twelve native sources under getdp/ are the GetDP problem definitions, kept byte for byte.

The backend refuses four things before it runs.

  • A Γ vector whose length differs from the number of frequencies.
  • A conductor of zero conductivity at the operating temperature.
  • A cable tangent to the ground surface.
  • A native path argument of 256 bytes or more.

Everything it writes goes under .linecablemodels/fem/ of the working directory, removed after the run unless keep_run_directory is set. The result is a LineParameters{Float64, FEM, N}. For any number type of the problem, the backend computes the nominal problem in Float64 and returns no uncertainty.

The extension is the owner of the native data. The string comparisons that decode Gmsh's and GetDP's outputs live in it and nowhere else.

Uncertainty propagation

using Measurements loads LineCableModelsMeasurementsExt. The extension is methods only. It defines nominal on a Measurement and four methods of the SpecialFunctions Bessel functions on an uncertain complex argument, with their derivative identities. It defines no name of its own. Everything else is the parametric T of the package. Γ and every option refuse a Measurement, because a control is not a physical input. The test tree checks the nominal parts against the deterministic engine and the sensitivities against finite differences.

Register and lexicon

test/architecture/names.toml is the one record of every name the package defines. It has two halves.

The lexicon lists the sanctioned terms, globally and per module, each with one role: verb, noun, qualifier, symbol or preposition. Each name splits into its terms in exactly one way. A function is a verb, a verb and its object, a quantity, a qualifier, or a predicate, in at most two terms. A field, keyword, constant or module is a noun phrase of at most two terms. A type is a noun phrase of at most three terms after Abstract. Each rejected word has a stated replacement: check is validate, get is the name of the quantity, data is the name of what it stores.

The register lists, per module and per extension, every function, type and constant the module defines, as public or private, each with its meaning. The families list their identifiers with a one-line reference each. The lists lifts mark the functions that lift a value to a type with a Val.

[register."Engine.EarthReturn".formulas]
unified = "the circumferentially averaged earth return of air, earth and mixed systems ..."

A name enters the register with the maintainers' confirmation, in the change that introduces it, and leaves with its definition. Everything else is a method of a registered function or type, or of a function of Base, a standard library or a dependency.

A set of planted probes, the negative controls, proves that each guard reports a violation. Beside the guards, Aqua checks the project, JET and @inferred check the inference of compute, and the loop's allocation is measured exactly. Under test/references/, six frozen reference cases hold the physics to finite-element results.
